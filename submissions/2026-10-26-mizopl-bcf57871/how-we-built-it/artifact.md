# Dual-Encoded DRAT/LRAT-Certified Forced-Structure Reduction and Automorphism Exclusions for Conway's 99-Graph Problem ($\mathrm{srg}(99, 14, 1, 2)$)

## Overview

Conway's 99-graph problem asks whether there exists a strongly regular graph $\Gamma = \mathrm{srg}(99, 14, 1, 2)$, equivalently a graph on $99$ vertices in which every edge lies in a unique triangle ($\lambda = 1$) and every non-adjacent pair of vertices shares a unique $4$-cycle ($\mu = 2$).

This artifact provides standalone, zero-dependency machine-checked UNSAT certificates (in both **DRAT** and **LRAT** formats, with **0 RAT lemmas**, i.e., 100% pure Reverse Unit Propagation) across **two independent SAT cardinality encodings**—**Encoding A** (Sinz 2005 Linear Sequential Counter) and **Encoding B** (Bailleux & Boufkhad 2003 Binary Tree Unary Totalizer)—for both published partial-progress tracks of the `conway-99-graph` target:

1. **Track `p2` (Forced-Structure Reduction of `arXiv:2608.11211`):**
   An independent machine-checked re-derivation of all five forced-structure reduction propositions (`p2_1`–`p2_5`) of Thakkar & Severini (`arXiv:2608.11211`, *Structure forced by Conway's 99-graph problem*, v2, 18 Sep 2026, Section 2.3), certifying how the global $\mathrm{srg}(99, 14, 1, 2)$ axioms around a fixed root vertex $x$ force the exact reduced constraints on the $84$-vertex second neighborhood $\Gamma_2(x)$, together with exactness and uniqueness on the smaller validation benchmark $\mathrm{srg}(9, 4, 1, 2) \cong K_3 \square K_3$.
2. **Track `p1` (DRAT/LRAT-Checked Prime-Order and Subgroup Automorphism Exclusions):**
   Machine-checked DRAT and LRAT UNSAT certificates (independently generated and verified under both Encoding A and Encoding B) proving that no $\mathrm{srg}(99, 14, 1, 2)$ admits $8$ specified prime-order ($p = 3$ and $p = 2$ with stated fixed-point and cycle structures) and order-$21$ Frobenius ($\mathrm{Frob}(21) = \mathbb{Z}_7 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_3$, generated by prime orders $p = 7$ and $p = 3$) automorphism actions whose previous exclusions in Wilbrink (1983), Crnković, Maksimović & Švob (`arXiv:2308.02978`, 2023), and Thakkar & Severini (`arXiv:2608.11211`, 2026) relied on custom backtracking or solver runs without published DRAT/LRAT proof certificates.

## Independent Verification (`verify.py`)

Running `python3 -B verify.py` executes the complete verification pipeline using only the Python standard library in $\sim 10$ seconds:
1. Re-generates all $26$ full CNF clause sets from scratch across Encoding A and Encoding B,
2. Verifies positive non-vacuity on $\mathrm{srg}(9, 4, 1, 2) \cong K_3 \square K_3$ by unit propagation on the unblocked Encoding A and Encoding B formulas,
3. Verifies that every clause in each of the $26$ trimmed `*_core.cnf` files is an exact member of the freshly generated CNF from its encoder,
4. Verifies all SHA-256 hashes, variable/clause counts, and cycle-structure invariants against `certificate.json`,
5. Runs a self-contained pure-Python LRAT RUP proof checker across all $26$ LRAT proofs ($9,816 + 9,877 = 19,693$ RUP steps, $0$ RAT lemmas), confirming derivation of the empty clause `0`.
