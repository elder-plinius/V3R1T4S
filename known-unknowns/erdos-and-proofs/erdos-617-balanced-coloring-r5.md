# Erdos #617 (Erdos-Gyarfas): balanced 5-colouring of K_26

✏️ **Erdős problems and proofs** · harvest (many cells or partial wins within reach) · tractability ★★☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-hours · open as of 2026-10-03

## The problem

Is there a 5-colouring of the edges of K_26 in which every 6-vertex set sees all 5 colours? Equivalently: can E(K_26) be partitioned into 5 graphs G_1..G_5, each with independence number alpha(G_c) <= 5? Erdos-Gyarfas conjecture that for every r >= 3 no balanced r-colouring of K_{r^2+1} exists. Per erdosproblems.com/617 (last edited 2026-04-01): proved for r = 3 and r = 4 (credited there to Erdos-Gyarfas); false for r = 2. On r^2 vertices balanced colourings exist for infinitely many r (affine planes). On 2026-07-15 the forum (42things) said r = 5 'appears to be open'. Status as of 2026-10-16: r = 5 is no longer open. The forum's proof-claims page lists four independent proof claims for r = 5: Rob Sneiderman (2026-07-18; Kang-Pikhurko bounds force every colour class to exactly 65 edges), Conner Silverstein (2026-07-21; a 66-edge lemma plus twelve UNSAT formulas checked with drat-trim), Anthony Rose (2026-07-25; least-colour stripping plus 458 DRAT-checked SAT leaves) and Nick Winter (2026-07-31; vertex-deletion argument, formalised in Lean). RamazanKara/erdos-617-r5-formal-verification (last pushed 2026-10-09) reports an unconditional Lean theorem for r = 5, formalising Sneiderman's argument. None of these has completed independent expert review. The general conjecture stays open; Sneiderman also claims r = 6 (K_37), r = 7 (K_50), r = 8 (K_65) and r = 9 (K_82), none of them formalised.

## What counts as a win

(A) An explicit symmetric 26x26 matrix with off-diagonal entries in {0..4} in which every one of the 230,230 six-sets sees all 5 colours. This disproves #617 for r=5 and is a full success. (B) A complete impossibility proof for r=5: an UNSAT result on an encoding regenerated by the verifier, with a DRAT/LRAT proof checked by drat-trim or cake_lpr, and every symmetry-breaking or structural lemma proved in writing and refereed. The same pipeline must first reproduce r=3 (K_10) and r=4 (K_17) UNSAT. Solving only a symmetric sub-family (Cayley colourings, etc.) is a partial win. Under (B), state explicitly that the conjecture stays open for r >= 6.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** A DRAT/LRAT-checked impossibility proof (CNF regenerated by the verifier, pipeline also reproducing r = 3 and r = 4 UNSAT) that no balanced 5-colouring of K_26 is a Cayley colouring on Z_26, i.e. colour depends only on the difference class (all 5^13 assignments excluded).
- **p2:** The same kind of checked impossibility for another named symmetric family stated in advance: Cayley colourings on Z_13 x Z_2 or D_13, or colourings by orbits of a named subgroup of PGammaL(2,25) acting on pairs of PG(1,25).

## How a win is checked

(A) Exhaustive loop over all six-sets with a colour bitmask check, plus an independent check that every colour class has independence number <=5 (exact max-clique of its complement) and >=55 edges. (B) Fresh encoding by the verifier; independent solver run; proof checking; manual audit of symmetry breaking (vertex lex-leader composed with colour permutation must be shown sound jointly); r=3 and r=4 sanity reproductions.

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Turan counting gives rigid structure. Each G_c has alpha <= 5, so its complement is K_6-free with at most t_5(26) = 270 edges, so |E(G_c)| >= 55. That totals 275 of the 325 edges, leaving only 50 'surplus' edges. By Erdos-Simonovits stability, each colour class is close to a disjoint union of cliques of sizes 6,5,5,5,5. Enumerate these clique-partition 'skeletons' plus the surplus edges; this may give a human-readable impossibility proof (probably how r=3 and r=4 were done by hand).
- Algebraic ansatz: 26 = |PG(1,25)|. Colour edges by orbits of subgroups of PGammaL(2,25) on unordered pairs. Also try Cayley colourings on Z_26, Z_13 x Z_2 and D_13, where the colour depends only on the difference class (13 classes, 5^13 ~ 1.2e9 assignments, pruned by exact alpha checks with numba; exhaustive search is feasible).
- Affine-plane extension: AG(2,5) (6 parallel classes merged into 5 colours) gives balanced colourings of K_25. Analyse exactly why adding a 26th point fails, and whether twisted or non-Desarguesian-like structures on 25+1 points escape.
- SAT with 1,625 variables and about 1.15M length-15 clauses: lex-leader symmetry breaking on S_26 (vertex order) and S_5 (colours), cube-and-conquer on 10 cores. In parallel, run MaxSAT or tabu search minimising the number of colour-deficient 6-sets, to hunt for (A).

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- Confusing K_{r^2} (where balanced colourings do exist via affine planes) with K_{r^2+1}.
- Reversing the quantifier: the conjecture says some 6-set MISSES a colour.
- Unsound composition of vertex and colour symmetry breaking in an UNSAT claim.
- Reporting a solver timeout as UNSAT.
- Estimating alpha heuristically instead of exactly.
- Attributions: the problem page credits r=3,4 to Erdos-Gyarfas; do not cite Chung-Liu without opening the source.
- Restricted-family UNSAT (e.g. no Z_26-Cayley colouring works) says nothing about general colourings.
- The r = 5 case was settled by proof claims posted in July 2026 (see statement): a new r = 5 impossibility proof is a reproduction, not a first. The problem page itself shows no status change; read the forum's proof-claims page and the formalisation repository before attacking.
