# Self-contained continuation proof for $0 \le m \le 12$

Write the sorted P-positions in layer $m$ as $(m, Y_j, Z_j)$, indexed from $j = 1$ in increasing order of $Y_j$. In Sun and Zeilberger's notation (*Ann. Comb.* 8 (2004), 225–238), $[m, A^m_j, B^m_j]$ has $A^m_j = Y_j - m$ and $B^m_j = Z_j - Y_j$. All floor evaluations $\lfloor k\phi \rfloor = \lfloor (k + \sqrt{5k^2})/2 \rfloor$ use exact integer arithmetic (`(k + isqrt(5*k*k)) // 2`).

## 1. Closed box, mex completeness, and $T^m$

In Fraenkel's three-heap Wythoff game, a legal move decreases one coordinate or subtracts a nonzero nonnegative triple $(d_1, d_2, d_3)$ with $d_1 \oplus d_2 \oplus d_3 = 0$. From any position $(m, Y, Z)$ with $m \le Y \le Z$, at most one coordinate exceeds $Y$, so every legal destination after sorting has middle coordinate at most $Y$ and largest coordinate at most $Z$. Hence any downward-closed max-heap box $Z \le M$ or complete-row horizon $Y \le M$ is closed under moves.

In layer $m$, the verifier computes $T^m = \{ Z' - m : (i, m, Z') \in P^i \text{ for some } i < m \}$ (Claim 3.1) and checks at every row up to the horizon that $Y_j = \operatorname{mex}(\{0,\dots,m-1\} \cup (m + T^m) \cup \{Y_r, Z_r : r < j\})$ and that $Z_j$ is not already in that set. Any skipped integer $y \ge m$ is either an earlier $Z_r$ (allowing a single-heap reduction from $(m, y, z)$ to $(m, Y_r, y)$) or $m + t$ for $t \in T^m$ (allowing a single-heap reduction to $(i, m, m+t)$). Thus no P-position with $Y \le Y_{\text{cutoff}}$ is omitted in any layer $0 \le m \le 12$, even if a skipped row had $Z$ large.

## 2. Offset bounds via Lemma 2.6 (gap-indexed Wythoff sequence)

Fix layer $m$ and let `start` $= 2$ for $m = 0$ and `start` $= N_1^m$ for $m \ge 1$ be the verified start of consecutive gaps $B^m_{j+1} = B^m_j + 1$. Define the finite forbidden set
$$Q = \{0, \dots, m - 1\} \cup (m + T^m) \cup \{Y^m_r, Z^m_r : 1 \le r < \text{start}\}.$$
From $g = B^m_{\text{start}}$ onward, define a synthetic sequence $(Y(g), Z(g))$ indexed by actual gap $g$ via $Y(g) = \operatorname{mex}(Q \cup \{Y(h), Z(h) : B^m_{\text{start}} \le h < g\})$ and $Z(g) = Y(g) + g$. Since $Z^m_{\text{start}} > \max Q$ and all gaps before `start` are strictly smaller than $B^m_{\text{start}}$, $Y(g)$ and $Z(g)$ increase monotonically and stay strictly above $\max Q$. This is a Wythoff sequence in the sense of Sun–Zeilberger Definition 2.1.

The verifier matches the exact game prefix through anchor $n_1 > n_2$ to this synthetic sequence and checks the finite history bounds required for Lemma 2.6:
- $Y^m_{n_2 - 3} > \max Q$, $Y^m_{n_1 - 3} > Z^m_{\text{first\_above}}$, $Y^m_{n_1 - 3} - 1 > Z^m_{n_2 - 5}$, and $Y^m_{n_1} > Z^m_{n_2}$, where `first_above` is the smallest index with $Y^m_j > \max Q$;
- $\varepsilon^m_j = Y^m_j - \lfloor B^m_j \phi \rfloor - \alpha^m \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ for $n_2 - 4 \le j \le n_1$, with $\varepsilon^m_{n_1} = 0$ and $\varepsilon^m_{j-2}\varepsilon^m_{j-1}\varepsilon^m_j = 0$ for $n_2 - 2 \le j \le n_1$.

Above $\max Q$, every integer skipped by $Y(g)$ is an earlier $Z(h)$, and consecutive $Z(h)$ differ by at least $2$, so middle steps $Y(g) - Y(g-1) \in \{1, 2\}$ and upper steps $Z(g) - Z(g-1) \in \{2, 3\}$. Above $Z(\text{first\_above})$, two consecutive middle steps of $1$ cannot occur because that would force an earlier upper gap of at least $4$. Comparing with the classical complementary Beatty sequences $W_g = \lfloor g\phi \rfloor$ and $V_g = W_g + g$ (whose $W$-steps are $1, 2$ with no consecutive $1$s and no three consecutive $2$s), the same minimality argument as Sun–Zeilberger Lemma 2.6 rules out any first violation $|\varepsilon| = 2$ or any three consecutive nonzero errors $(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)$ for all $g > B^m_{n_1}$. In particular, $|\varepsilon^m_j| \le 1$ and $\varepsilon^m_{j-2}\varepsilon^m_{j-1}\varepsilon^m_j = 0$ hold for every synthetic term $j > n_1$.

## 3. Missing gaps ($S_1^m(n_1)$) and infinite ray witnesses (Claim 3.3)

For each layer $1 \le m \le 12$ and each missing gap $c \in S^m = \{0, \dots, B^m_{n_1} - 1\} \setminus \{B^m_1, \dots, B^m_{n_1}\}$, `wythoff_a1_12_certificate.json` records a verified lower-layer P-position $[a, A_0, B_0]$ ($0 \le a < m$) for every $Y \in [Y^m_{n_1}, Y^m_{n_1} + p(m)]$ where $p(m) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor + 1}$. In every witness, some coordinate permutation $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ of $(a, a+A_0, a+A_0+B_0)$ satisfies $u_1 \le m$, $u_2 \le Y$, $u_3 \le Y + c$, and $d = m - u_1$ with $0 \le d < p(m)$, $e = Y - u_2 \ge 0$, $f = Y + c - u_3 \ge 0$, $d + e + f > 0$, and $d \oplus e \oplus f = 0$.

Because $0 \le d < p(m)$ and $e \oplus f = d$, the high bits $\lfloor e / p(m) \rfloor$ and $\lfloor f / p(m) \rfloor$ are identical. Adding any integer multiple $k \cdot p(m)$ ($k \ge 0$) to $Y$ therefore adds $k \cdot p(m)$ to both $e$ and $f$, preserving nonnegativity, $d + e + f > 0$, and $d \oplus e \oplus f = 0$. Hence every position $(m, Y, Y + c)$ with $Y \ge Y^m_{n_1}$ and $c \in S^m$ can move in one step to a fixed lower-layer P-position, proving $S_1^m(n_1) = S^m$ for all larger $Y$.

## 4. Direct induction on $m = 1, \dots, 12$ (avoiding the p. 235 midpoint typo)

We prove by induction on $m = 1, \dots, 12$ (with $m = 0$ being classical Wythoff's game) that the synthetic tail from $n_1$ onward equals the true P-position sequence $P^m$. Assume this holds for all $0 \le i < m$ and through gap $g - 1 \ge B^m_{n_1}$ in layer $m$, and consider the proposed next pair $(m, Y(g), Y(g) + g)$:

1. **No same-layer move**: Single-heap moves preserve two heaps, impossible since $Y(g)$ is new by mex and $Y(g) + g$ strictly exceeds all earlier $Z$. Same-layer XOR moves require equal reductions of the two larger heaps, preserving the gap $g$, which has not occurred before.
2. **No lower-layer move with two small heaps**: Every lower-layer P-position $(i, u, v)$ ($i < m$) with $u \le m$ has $v \le \text{small\_two\_max}(m)$ (verified: $34$ for $m=11$, $36$ for $m=12$). Since $Y(g) > \text{small\_two\_max}(m)$, no single-heap move can reach such a position. Since $g > \text{small\_two\_max}(m) + m$ and any XOR move with first reduction $d \le m$ changes the signed gap by $|e - f| \le e \oplus f = d \le m$, no XOR move can reach such a position either.
3. **No lower-layer move with smallest heap $i$ in the first coordinate**: For any other lower P-position $(i, Y_i(h), Y_i(h) + h)$ with $i < m$, we must have $Y_i(h) > m$, so its smallest heap $i$ must occupy the physical first coordinate (reduction $d = m - i \ge 1$). A single-heap move on the first heap keeps $h = g$, and an XOR move requires signed gap $\pm h \ge g - d$, hence $h \ge g - d$ in either ordering of the larger two heaps. Since $B^m_{n_1} > \max_{i < m} (B^i_{\max\text{-early}} + m - i)$ and $B^m_{n_1} > \max_{i < m} B^i_{N_3^i + 1}$, Lemma 2.6 applies to both $Y_m(g)$ and $Y_i(h)$:
   $$Y_i(h) - Y_m(g) \ge \lfloor h\phi \rfloor + \alpha^i - 1 - (\lfloor g\phi \rfloor + \alpha^m + 1) \ge \alpha^i - \alpha^m - \lceil d\phi \rceil - 2 = \alpha^i - \alpha^m - \lfloor d\phi \rfloor - 3 = \text{slack}(m, i).$$
   - For $m \in \{11, 12\}$ (and whenever $\text{slack}(m, i) > 0$), $Y_i(h) > Y_m(g)$ immediately, contradicting $Y_i(h) \le Y_m(g)$ required by either assignment of the larger two heaps.
   - For $1 \le m \le 10$, the verifier checks $\text{slack}(m, i) \ge 0$, with $\text{slack}(m, i) = 0$ occurring only when $d = m - i = 1$ and $\alpha^{m-1} - \alpha^m = 4$. Equality $Y_{m-1}(h) = Y_m(g)$ then forces $h = g - 1$, $\lfloor g\phi \rfloor - \lfloor (g-1)\phi \rfloor = 2$, $\varepsilon^m_g = +1$, and $\varepsilon^{m-1}_{g-1} = -1$. By Lemma 2.6 past $B^{m-1}_{N_3^{m-1}}$, $\varepsilon^m_{g-1} = 0$, which forces $Y_m(g) - Y_m(g-1) = 3$, impossible because above $\max Q$ the mex sequence skips only earlier upper heaps (separated by at least $2$) and hence has steps $\le 2$. Thus $Y_i(h) > Y_m(g)$ strictly in all cases.
4. **Blocking of all smaller gaps**: Every gap $c < g$ at middle heap $Y(g)$ is either already used (allowing an equal 2-heap reduction $(0, k, k)$ to the earlier same-gap P-position) or lies in $S^m$ (blocked by its Section 3 ray witness). Any larger gap $z - Y(g) > g$ can reduce the third heap to $(m, Y(g), Y(g) + g)$. Finally, any middle heap skipped by the mex after $Y(g-1)$ is blocked by a single-heap move to an earlier $Z$ or to $m + T^m$.

Therefore $(m, Y(g), Y(g) + g)$ is the exact next P-position for every $g > B^m_{n_1}$, proving Conjecture 1 ($B^m_{j+1} = B^m_j + 1$ and the mex rule) and Conjecture 2 ($|\varepsilon^m_j| \le 1$) for every $0 \le m \le 12$, independently of Claim 3.5's misprinted p. 235 formula. Moreover, under the intended half-sum $\alpha' = \lfloor (\max + \min)/2 \rfloor$, all eight hypotheses of Claim 3.5 and all hypotheses of Claims 3.3 and 3.4 hold simultaneously for every $1 \le m \le 12$.
