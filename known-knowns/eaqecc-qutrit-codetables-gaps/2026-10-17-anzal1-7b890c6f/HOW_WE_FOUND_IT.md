# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

## The brief
We targeted Markus Grassl's qutrit entanglement-assisted quantum error-correcting code table (`https://eaqecc.codetables.de/qutrit.php`, bank id `eaqecc-qutrit-codetables-gaps`). Scouting the 3,888 cells of `qutrit.php` along constant-stabilizer-rank diagonals `s = n - k + c = 2(n - M)` revealed a sharp drop at `n = 14, s = 16` (`D = [14, 6]_9` over `GF(9)`): while `[[14, 6, 8-9; 8]]_3` (`ell = 0`), `[[14, 5, 8-9; 7]]_3` (`ell = 1`), and `[[14, 4, 8-9; 6]]_3` (`ell = 2`) all had `d_lower = 8` (obtained by twice quantum-shortening `[[16, 2, 10; 6]]_3` from Luo, Ezerman, Grassl, and Ling, `arXiv:2207.05647`, which landed at hull dimension `ell = 2`), the `ell = 3` cell `[[14, 3, 7-10; 5]]_3` dropped to `d_lower = 7` (`reducing length of [[19,3,7;0]] from codetables.de`) because Theorem 4's hull-decreasing rule can only step from `ell = 2` down to `ell = 1, 0`, never up to `ell = 3`.

## What failed
- **Truncated `8 x 8` and unitary `7 x 7` $\lambda$-circulants:** Scanning `[I_6 | A_8]` where `A_8` is the first 6 rows of an `8 x 8` $\lambda$-circulant over `GF(9)` produced 15 distinct `[14, 6, 8]_9` codes in under a second, but all 15 had maximum hull dimension `ell = 2` (`min_rank = 4`) across all `2^13` diagonal column scalings `b in {1, 2}^14`. Exhaustively scanning all `531,441` unitary `7 x 7` $\lambda$-circulants capped at `d = 6`.
- **Sequential row-by-row greedy/annealed extension (`find_pure_14_6_8.c`) and Proposition 6 chains (`chain_prop6.c`):** Iterating Proposition 6 (`pextendrule` of `arXiv:2207.05647`) from `[11, 3, 8]_9` (`ell = 3`) reached `[12, 4, 8]_9` (`ell = 4`) and `[13, 5, 8]_9` (`ell = 4`), and greedy row addition over `GF(9)^14` reached `[14, 5, 8]_9` on 14 consecutive trials in `< 0.1s` each, but stalled on the 6th row because non-symmetric 5-row prefixes form complete caps.

## What worked
- **2-Quasi-Cyclic Reciprocal-Factor Decomposition over `GF(9)[x]/(x^7 - 1)` (`how-we-built-it/search_qc14_6_8.c`):** Over `GF(3)` and `GF(9)`, `x^7 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^3 + x^2 + 2)(x^3 + 2x + 2)`, where the two cubic factors `p_1(x)` and `p_2(x)` are Hermitian-reciprocal (`-x^3 p_1(x^{-1}) = p_2(x)`). Consequently, the 3-dimensional ideals `C_1 = <(x-1)p_2(x)>` and `C_2 = <(x-1)p_1(x)>` inside `GF(9)[x]/(x^7 - 1)` are each identically Hermitian self-orthogonal. Searching 1-generator 2-quasi-cyclic `[14, 6]_9` codes in `<(x-1)> x <(x-1)>` found on Hit #18 (`< 0.1s`) a **pure, Hermitian self-orthogonal (`ell = 6`) `[14, 6, 8]_9` code**.
- **Hull-dimension calibration via Theorem 4 (`how-we-built-it/build_14_3_8_5.py`):** Multiplying columns `0, 1, 2` of the `[14, 6, 8]_9` self-orthogonal seed by `4 = 1 + w in GF(9)^*` (which has Hermitian norm `2 = -1 mod 3`) reduces `dim(Hull_H(D))` from `ell = 6` to `ell = 3` (`rank(H_9 H_9^†) = 3 = k`, `c = 5`) while preserving the exact minimum distance `d = 8`, yielding the pure `[[14, 3, 8; 5]]_3` stabilizer matrix `S` (`object/qutrit_14_3_8_5.json`).
