# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

Method: a computer search for the largest code invariant under a prescribed group, the orbit method of Laaksonen and Ostergard: pick a group of permutations of the coordinates and the symbols, split the space of words into orbits, and solve a maximum-weight clique problem over the orbits. Each group acts on words by permuting the coordinates and permuting the symbols (the same symbol permutation at every coordinate, or, for some families, a different one at one coordinate). The search makes the orbits of the group on all q^n words, keeps the orbits whose words are pairwise at distance >= d, joins two orbits when every word of one is at distance >= d from every word of the other, and looks for a maximum-weight clique (weight = orbit size) among the cliques of weight at least a target. A line marked COMPLETE with best=-1 means that no code invariant under that group reaches the target; COMPLETE with best=B means the largest such code has exactly B words; TIMEOUT and TOO_MANY_VERTICES lines establish nothing. The search programs are in how-we-built-it/src/ and the logs in how-we-built-it/logs/ (one line per group).

Groups sampled. (1) Every conjugacy class of subgroups of AGL(1, Z_15) x S_3 (coordinate maps i -> a i + b mod 15 with a a unit, times all permutations of the three symbols), 232 classes, searched with target 32 (logs/classes_target32.tsv; orders 1 to 720): 187 finished, 44 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap), 1 stopped at the time limit; no group reached 32. (2) 218 subgroups sampled at random from 22 families of larger coordinate groups (cyclic and affine groups on 15 or 14+1 points, PGL(2,13) on 13+1 points plus a fixed point, GL(4,2) on the 15 points of PG(3,2), S_6 on the 15 pairs of a 6-set, S_3 x S_5 on a 3 x 5 grid; with several kinds of symbol action), searched with target 31 (logs/sampled_target31.tsv, the plan rows in logs/plan_rows.tsv): 182 finished, 36 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap); 21 of them reached exactly 31 and none more.

The code: the zero word and one orbit of 30 words of weight 11 under the group of order 30 generated by the cyclic shift and the exchange of the symbols 1 and 2 (one generator, the shift combined with the exchange, already generates it: class 98 of logs/classes_target32.tsv, line `(1i+1;021)`). For that group the search is complete in both runs: no invariant code reaches 32, and the target-31 run found 31. So 31 is the largest code invariant under this group, according to the search log. The same holds for the cyclic group of order 15 alone (class 149, `(1i+1;012)`, complete, nothing at 32; the target-31 run found 31).

Maximality: no word can be added. how-we-built-it/maximal.c scans all 3^15 words and counts those at distance >= 10 from every codeword; it reports 0 (how-we-built-it/MAXIMALITY.txt). As a check of the scanner, the same run with the first codeword removed reports that word as addable.

## What was not established

- Not optimal: the table's upper bound is 39, and no argument here says that 31 is the largest possible code. Codes not invariant under a sampled group were not searched.
- "Largest for this group" comes from the search log (a COMPLETE line of the exact clique search), not from an independent recomputation.
- The group samples are samples: they cover the listed classes and families, not all groups.
- Timings and parallel settings of the original runs are in the logs where recorded; the time limit and vertex cap of the class-by-class runs were not recorded.
