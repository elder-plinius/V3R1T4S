# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

Method: a computer search for the largest code invariant under a prescribed group, the orbit method of Laaksonen and Ostergard: pick a group of permutations of the coordinates and the symbols, split the space of words into orbits, and solve a maximum-weight clique problem over the orbits. Each group acts on words by permuting the coordinates and permuting the symbols (the same symbol permutation at every coordinate, or, for some families, a different one at one coordinate). The search makes the orbits of the group on all q^n words, keeps the orbits whose words are pairwise at distance >= d, joins two orbits when every word of one is at distance >= d from every word of the other, and looks for a maximum-weight clique (weight = orbit size) among the cliques of weight at least a target. A line marked COMPLETE with best=-1 means that no code invariant under that group reaches the target; COMPLETE with best=B means the largest such code has exactly B words; TIMEOUT and TOO_MANY_VERTICES lines establish nothing. The search programs are in how-we-built-it/src/ and the logs in how-we-built-it/logs/ (one line per group).

Groups sampled (length 12, d = 8). (1) 160 subgroups sampled at random from 20 families of coordinate groups (cyclic and affine groups on 12 or 11+1 points, PGL(2,11), M12, S_2 x S_6 and S_3 x S_4 on grids) with several kinds of symbol action, searched with target 128 (logs/sampled_12_8_target128.tsv): 98 finished, 61 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap), 1 stopped at the time limit. One group reached 145 and one 144. (2) A second sample of 107 subgroups, weighted toward orders 24 to 72 and adding symbol actions that fix the symbol 0 at every coordinate, searched with target 145 (logs/sampled_12_8_target145.tsv): 103 finished, 4 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap); 3 reached exactly 145 and none more. Plan rows: logs/plan_rows.tsv.

The (12,145,8) code: the union of 5 orbits (sizes 1, 36, 36, 36, 36) of the group of order 36 generated by A (i -> 5i+1 mod 12) and B (i -> i+2 mod 12 with symbols 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 1, 0 fixed); its line in logs/sampled_12_8_target128.tsv (family agl12/glob, subgroup g2) reads COMPLETE best=145, so 145 is the largest code invariant under this group, according to the search log. The group fixes the symbol 0 at every coordinate, so it does not act transitively on the 48 (coordinate, symbol) pairs.

Maximality: no word can be added. how-we-built-it/maximal.c scans all 4^12 words and counts those at distance >= 8 from every codeword; it reports 0 (how-we-built-it/MAXIMALITY.txt). As a check of the scanner, the same run with the first codeword removed reports that word as addable.

## What was not established

- Not optimal: the table's upper bound is 240, and no argument here says that 145 is the largest possible code. Codes not invariant under a sampled group were not searched.
- "Largest for this group" comes from the search log (a COMPLETE line of the exact clique search), not from an independent recomputation.
- The group samples are samples: they cover the listed classes and families, not all groups.
- Timings and parallel settings of the original runs are in the logs where recorded; the time limit and vertex cap of the class-by-class runs were not recorded.
