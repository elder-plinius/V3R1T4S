# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

Method: a computer search for the largest code invariant under a prescribed group, the orbit method of Laaksonen and Ostergard: pick a group of permutations of the coordinates and the symbols, split the space of words into orbits, and solve a maximum-weight clique problem over the orbits. Each group acts on words by permuting the coordinates and permuting the symbols (the same symbol permutation at every coordinate, or, for some families, a different one at one coordinate). The search makes the orbits of the group on all q^n words, keeps the orbits whose words are pairwise at distance >= d, joins two orbits when every word of one is at distance >= d from every word of the other, and looks for a maximum-weight clique (weight = orbit size) among the cliques of weight at least a target. A line marked COMPLETE with best=-1 means that no code invariant under that group reaches the target; COMPLETE with best=B means the largest such code has exactly B words; TIMEOUT and TOO_MANY_VERTICES lines establish nothing. The search programs are in how-we-built-it/src/ and the logs in how-we-built-it/logs/ (one line per group).

Groups sampled. (1) Every conjugacy class of subgroups of AGL(1, Z_12) x S_4 of order at least 8 (coordinate maps i -> a i + b mod 12 with a in {1, 5, 7, 11}, times all permutations of the four symbols), 2432 classes, searched with target 257 (logs/classes_target257.tsv): 1251 finished, 963 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap), 218 stopped at the time limit. Two groups, both of order 48, reached 320 and none more. (2) 204 subgroups sampled at random from 21 families of larger coordinate groups (cyclic and affine groups on 12 or 11+1 points, PGL(2,11) on 12 points, the Mathieu group M12, S_2 x S_6 and S_3 x S_4 on grids; several kinds of symbol action), searched with target 320 (logs/sampled_target320.tsv, plan rows in logs/plan_rows.tsv): 134 finished, 67 skipped (more orbit vertices than the cap), 3 stopped at the time limit; none reached 320.

The code: the union of 11 orbits of the group of order 48 of class 530 in logs/classes_target257.tsv, generators `(1i+2;0231) (11i+0;1023)`: coordinate i -> i+2 with symbols 0123 -> 0231, and i -> 11i with symbols 0123 -> 1023. Its line reads COMPLETE best=320, so 320 is the largest code invariant under this group, according to the search log. The other order-48 hit (class 672, `(1i+0;1032) (1i+2;0123) (11i+0;0213)`, 10 orbits) also gave 320 words with the same distance distribution; whether the two codes are equivalent was not tested.

Maximality: no word can be added. how-we-built-it/maximal.c scans all 4^12 words and counts those at distance >= 7 from every codeword; it reports 0 (how-we-built-it/MAXIMALITY.txt). As a check of the scanner, the same run with the first codeword removed reports that word as addable.

## What was not established

- Not optimal: the table's upper bound is 1167, and no argument here says that 320 is the largest possible code. Codes not invariant under a sampled group were not searched.
- "Largest for this group" comes from the search log (a COMPLETE line of the exact clique search), not from an independent recomputation.
- The group samples are samples: they cover the listed classes and families, not all groups.
- Timings and parallel settings of the original runs are in the logs where recorded; the time limit and vertex cap of the class-by-class runs were not recorded.
