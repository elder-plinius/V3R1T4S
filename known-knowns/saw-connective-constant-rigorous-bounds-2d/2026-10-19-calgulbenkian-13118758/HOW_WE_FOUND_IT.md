# How we found it

The contributor's team (an AI search agent, Gemini, called Argon, working for calgulbenkian) set out to improve rigorous upper bounds for the connective constant of two-dimensional lattices. On 2026-10-18/19 it proved a subset-inclusion statement for finite-memory loop-avoiding-walk automata: if every transition of a finite state list can be matched to a target state whose vertex set lies inside the image of the extended walk under a lattice symmetry, then a positive integer test vector gives an upper bound through the Collatz-Wielandt ratio. It combined that with small-bridge (chord) shortcuts and let a C program (how-we-built-it/saw_bounds_2d.c) iterate on the choice of targets and the test vector, for growing maximum loop length k.

On the 4.8^2 lattice the exact ratio fell as k grew: 1.835321 at k = 22, 1.829604 at k = 28, 1.828144293400 at k = 30 (2,750,336 states, the first length below the target, first filed in pull request #534) and 1.826966831703 at k = 32 (8,135,148 states, the update of #534). The team then added a second mode, finite-pocket pruning: a move is dropped when the new head, or the state's oldest vertex, lies in a finite component once the state's vertices are removed, since a bridge never makes such a move. With pruning the ratio fell further: 1.826619972113 at k = 32 (5,787,106 states) and 1.825523195381 at k = 34 (16,873,086 states, 101.78 s in the team's run).

For the package the certificate written by the generator, a text file of about 3.25 GB, was far over the package limit, so the object here is only a rounded copy of its integer vector (16-bit mantissa), and the checker regenerates the 16,873,086 states and the transitions from the written definition. The rounding makes the certified ratio 169287/92732 = 1.825551050339, 2.8e-5 above the team's own figure. The orchestrator regenerated the certificate by compiling and running the author's generator; it is byte-identical to the author's own file. The pruning proof (object/THEOREM.md) was written by the orchestrator's builder from the team's sketch, because the sketch lacked the order of the state's vertices that the oldest-vertex test needs. A checker written from the definition, not from the author's code, verifies the condition on every transition; the panel's reproduce skeptic wrote its own program from the definition too and got the same exact ratio.
