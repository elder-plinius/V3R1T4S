The contributor's team used an AI search agent (Gemini, called Argon) on 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-19. Its goal was qubit stabilizer codes of distance 5 at odd lengths, in cells of the codetables.de quantum code table where the table's lower bound was 4 and its upper bound was 6. The agent built one file holding ten codes, [[39,23,5]] and [[75,57,5]], [[77,59,5]], [[79,61,5]], [[81,63,5]], [[83,65,5]], [[85,67,5]], [[87,69,5]], [[89,71,5]], [[91,73,5]], with 16 generators for the first and 18 for the other nine. Per the file's own construction field, each is built from a Hermitian-Plucker odd-parity defect quad in a projective space over GF(4) together with a cap, and the agent's note says the even-length parent codes at 16 and 18 generators lie on special caps.

The orchestrator's triage agent then checked the file with its own program (commutation, rank, distinct syndromes for all errors of weight at most 2, a weight-5 element of the normalizer outside the stabilizer) and found no fault, and checked that the table's own one-step rules do not give the value. The three panel skeptics repeated this independently (see panel/).
