# Criterion skeptic verdict, labs-merit-factor-records, L = 242

context: subagent (same VM)

confirmed_scope: The explicit +-1 sequence of length 242 in the claim folder has exact integer aperiodic energy E = 3337 (merit factor 58564/6674 = 8.7749), strictly below the baseline minimum of 3593 (Dimitrov et al. arXiv:2206.12070 table row, whose decoded sequence I recomputed to 3593; Knauer 2004 gives 4073) over the ten sources actually read (arXiv 1406.5301, 1512.02475, 2011.00068, 2106.03377, 2206.12070, 2210.15962, 2409.07222, 2504.00987, 2607.09688 and the Knauer 2004 table in the git_labs copy), with the Boskovic records even-length file, the arXiv listing and the journal-only papers unread, and the sequence being a 3-element end trim (1 off the front, 2 off the back) of the published L=245 record (E = 3226) of arXiv:2210.15962.

## Reasoning

Element-by-element check against the verbatim criterion:
- Explicit +-1 sequence: sequence.txt is 242 characters over {0,1} (0 -> +1, 1 -> -1). Yes.
- 67 <= L <= 600: L = 242. Yes.
- Exact integer energy: I recomputed with check_energy.py (pure integer arithmetic): 242 3337. I also recomputed the parent (245 characters, E = 3226, matching the printed value in 2210.15962) and confirmed that sequence.txt equals parent[1:-2], so the "how it was made" statement is accurate. The energy is odd, consistent with the parity constraint for even L (sum of C_k^2 with C_k = L-k mod 2).
- Strictly smaller than the best published energy for that L: 3337 < 3593, an integer comparison, margin 256 (F 8.775 vs 8.150). I decoded the 2206.12070 hex entry for 242 (61 hex digits, 242 bits after left padding) and got E = 3593, and F = 58564/7186 = 8.1497 matches the printed 8.14973559699. Knauer's printed 4073 matches its printed F 7.1893. So the baseline values are internally consistent and not rounding artefacts. The uncertainty is not in the comparison but in whether 3593 is really the minimum.
- One table cell: L = 242 only; no quantifier over lengths hidden in the claim. The claim does not say "record", "best known" or "new record in the literature"; it says strictly below the lowest energy published in the sources listed in baseline.tsv, which is the honest scope.

How well is the minimum established?
- Of the criterion's named sources, arXiv:2607.09688 (450..527 and 573) and arXiv:2504.00987 (92..120) were read and have no row for 242. The Boskovic records repository was only partly read: the Knauer 2004 table from the same repository is read and has a row (4073), but the even-length records file (Merit_Factor_Records-2013-even.html) returned 404 and the tree listing 403. That is the one unread source the criterion names explicitly and that could have an even L = 242 entry. It is a real hole, and the claim does not hide it (sources_checked.md lists it).
- "Any newer arXiv result" was not exhaustively searched (arXiv API 429, search page 429). Only the nine arXiv sources found via citations were read. A newer paper with an L = 242 row cannot be excluded.
- Journal-only versions (IEEE Access 2018, MENDEL 2022, DSP 2025, J. Comput. Sci. 2024) were unreadable. These are the likely publication homes of Boskovic17, Brest22 and Dimitrov follow-ups.
- Do the unread sources likely hold a better value? I judge it unlikely, not impossible, for these reasons. (1) The 2607.09688 statement, quoted in sources_checked.md, says local search in boskovic17 and dimitrov22 produced "merit factor exceeding 8.0 for 172 <= L <= 268"; the 2206.12070 entry for 242 (F 8.15) is consistent with that, and I see nothing in it suggesting a published F near 8.8 at this length (a claim of that size would normally be called out). (2) L = 242 is even, so it has no skew-symmetry; the skew-symmetric search that produced the 9.0 to 9.4 records at neighbouring odd lengths (2210.15962: 241 -> 9.03, 243 -> 9.13, 245 -> 9.30, 247 -> 9.36) does not apply, and the published even-length entry is itself an operator product (B_241 composed with eta_2, appended from a 241 record of F 8.07). Non-skew-symmetric direct search in this length range is not known from the read sources to exceed 8.x. (3) 2210.15962 covers odd lengths only and 2011.00068 (PSL tables) has no even rows near 242, so none of the read sources that reach F > 9 can cover L = 242 directly; a better published even-L value would have to come from derived-sequence work like this one. These are inferences, not evidence of absence.

Is an end-operator product of another length's record a genuine, non-subsumed result under the criterion as written?
- The criterion asks only for an explicit sequence of length L with exact energy strictly below the published best for that L. It contains no novelty-of-method clause, and it does not forbid derivation from another length's record. The baseline entry for 242 is itself produced by end operators from the L = 241 record, so the field treats this construction as a legitimate way to obtain a length-L entry.
- Subsumption check: no read table lists a 242 value from the 245 record, and trimming does not make the 245 row "a 242 row". The sequence is not equal to any printed sequence (different length, the L = 242 rows decode to 3593 and 4073). So it is not subsumed by any listed cell.
- Caveat that bounds the claim: the improvement comes almost entirely from the L = 245 record of 2210.15962, which appeared after the 2206.12070 table; the win says the 242 table row is stale relative to later odd-length records, not that a new search method or a new low-autocorrelation sequence discovery was made. The same 3-element trim (or similar) of other recent records may give comparably better entries for other even lengths; this is a single-length, single-cell result and should be described as "a trimmed published record beats the published L = 242 row", never as a new record discovery of a search procedure.
- The trim itself was apparently not optimised as a novelty (parent.txt gives one op), which is fine; but the claim must not say the 245 record's quality carries over: E rose from 3226 to 3337 and F fell from 9.30 to 8.77.

Hidden quantifiers and stretched definitions looked for, none found: "published energy" is handled by taking the minimum over printed energies, the smallest integer energy consistent with printed F, and the recomputed energy of each printed sequence (all agree for both rows); the old-MF column in 2206.12070 (7.1893) is Knauer's value and is correctly marked as having no sequence; no rounded merit-factor comparison is used; encoding direction is irrelevant to E.

Verdict: the object meets the full success criterion (explicit sequence, in range, exact integer energy, strictly below the minimum over every read published row for L = 242, one cell). The remaining doubt is the unread Boskovic even-length file, un-searched newer arXiv papers and journal-only versions; I judge a better unread published value unlikely but the confirmation holds only for the sources read, as the scope sentence says. It is a genuine win under the criterion as written, with the modest-novelty caveat above.
