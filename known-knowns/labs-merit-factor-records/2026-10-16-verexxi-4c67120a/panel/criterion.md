confirmed_scope: The explicit +-1 sequence of length 232 in the claim folder has exactly recomputed integer energy E = 3032 (F = 8.876), strictly below 3340 (F = 8.0575, Dimitrov et al. arXiv:2206.12070), which is the minimum over the rows for L = 232 in the ten sources actually read (Knauer 2004 copy in the Boskovic git_labs repository, 3752; 2206.12070, 3340; the other eight read sources, 1406.5301, 1512.02475, 2011.00068, 2106.03377, 2210.15962, 2409.07222, 2504.00987 and 2607.09688, have no row for 232), and it is not established against the unread repository pages, Mertens' table, journal-only versions or arXiv posts newer than 2607.09688.

Reasoning
- Elements checked literally. The sequence is explicit (232 characters over 0/1, i.e. +1/-1). L = 232 lies in 67..600. I recomputed E two ways (the supplied stdlib checker and a separate numpy sum of squared C_k, k = 1..231): E = 3032 both times, F = 53824/6064 = 8.876. The compared value is an integer. It is one table cell (L = 232), min direction. 3032 < 3340 is a strict integer inequality (margin 308, about 9.2 percent).
- Baseline check. I decoded the 2206.12070 hex string for L = 232 (removing its internal space, 232 bits) and recomputed E = 3340, which equals 53824/(2 x 8.05748502994). The 2206.12070 row therefore gives exactly 3340. The Knauer row gives 3752 (printed F 7.1727 matches 3752). The old-MF line in 2206.12070 is again 3752. So the minimum over read rows is 3340. Two of the three named bank tables (2607.09688: lengths 450..573; 2504.00987: lengths 92..120) have no row for 232, so the baseline for this length rests on the repository and wider literature, not on those two tables.
- Hidden quantifier / stretched definition. The claim text says "lowest energy published ... in the sources listed in baseline.tsv", which is the honest scope. The bank criterion says "best published energy for that L ... minimum over the tables ...", and the repository pages beyond the Knauer copy (even/odd Merit_Factor_Records pages, tree listing), Mertens' open.dat, the journal-only versions and an arXiv listing for post-2607.09688 results were NOT read (all documented as failed fetches). So "minimum over everything the criterion names" is not fully established; it is established over the read sources. I find no definitional stretch in E, F or the sequence encoding (E is invariant under global negation).
- Parity and coverage of L = 232. 232 is even, so no skew-symmetric object exists; the high-F published records (about 9.0-9.35) are for odd skew-symmetric lengths (2210.15962 covers odd 171..247 only; 2011.00068 is a PSL table of odd lengths 201..303; 1406.5301 has no 232 row). The only read published energies for even 232 are Knauer 2004 and Dimitrov 2022, where even-length values (8.0-8.7) are themselves derived by end operators from neighbouring odd skew-symmetric records of lower quality (232 = B_233 o eta_4). A published even-232 value with F above 8.876 would exceed the generic even-length level in the read literature by about 0.8 and would require someone to have applied the same operation to the late-2022 high-F odd records; the 2607.09688 quote (F > 8.0 for 172..268) is consistent with 8.06 and does not suggest more. I judge an unread better value unlikely but not excluded (rough guess under 10 percent); this residual risk is carried in the scope sentence rather than ignored.
- Null model. I reproduced the parent (229, E = 2810 recomputed; skew-symmetry verified; the sequence equals parent + '000'). All 32 possible 3-element end edits (any split of 3 added elements between the two ends, any signs) of the parent give mean E = 3500 (F about 7.7), std 273, minimum 3032, which is the claimed value; so the claim is the best of the 32, not a cherry-picked outlier from a larger unreported search, and the win is driven by the parent's quality, not by luck. Robustness: decoding the published odd records 231, 235, 237, 239 from 2210.15962 (the half-sequence hex agrees with the printed E for 229, 231, 235, 237, 239 only to the extent I recomputed them: 2810, 2963, 2965, 3118, 3055, matching the printed energies) and applying all end edits/trims to 232 gives best 3032 (from 229 and 231), 3100 (from 235 and 237) and 3808 (from 239). Every published record near 232 that I tried gives a result at or below 3100, hence below 3340; the conclusion does not hinge on one parent.
- Subsumption. The criterion as written asks for an explicit sequence of length L with exact energy strictly below the best published energy for that L. It places no requirement on how the sequence is found, and no read source prints a sequence or energy for L = 232 at or below 3032 (2210.15962 does not cover even lengths and says so by its table; 2206.12070 prints 3340). A derivative made by end operators from the L = 229 record is a different length with a different energy and is not itself a published row, so it is not subsumed by the parent's publication. It is, however, a modest, easy result: the operation is the very method (eta operators on neighbouring lengths) already used in 2206.12070 for even lengths, and the same energy 3032 is reached from two published parents, so the novelty is the application of that method to the later 2210.15962 records, not a new search. Under the criterion as written that still counts as a genuine win; the scope sentence must not be read as a claim of a new method or of a proven global record.
