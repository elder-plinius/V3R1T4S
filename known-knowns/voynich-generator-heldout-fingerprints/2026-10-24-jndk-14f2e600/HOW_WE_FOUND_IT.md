# How we found it

## 1. The brief and pre-registration
Five prior runs on `voynich-generator-heldout-fingerprints` investigated Naibbe failure modes (`arXiv:2608.17096` edge-glyph coupling and singleton rates, edit-1 neighbor rates, and `arXiv:2604.25979` `p3` per-folio `ch/sh`-vowel switching), leaving enumerated partial win `p2` (`arXiv:2604.19762` four Currier positional and sequential signatures across Currier A and B on both `ZL3b-n` and `IT2a-n`) completely untested. Before running any generator experiments, we froze `how-we-built-it/PREREG.md` (`2026-10-23T18:26:52Z`) specifying the 4-signature scorer (`verifier.py` and `signatures_v27.py` from `arXiv:2604.19762`), the 200-stream evaluation protocol (10 Latin + 10 Italian plaintexts $\times$ 10 seeds), the hand-executability ceiling ($\le 200$ table entries, $\le 1$ per-folio state bit), and the 400-control null suite (200 word-order permutations + 200 position-preserving glyph-shuffled controls per cell).

## 2. What failed in the published generators (Lens 1 `reader` & Lens 2 `obstruction`)
Running Parisel's official code (`signatures_v27.py`, `grille.py`, `verifier.py`) and Greshko's unmodified Naibbe cipher (`naibbe.py`) across `A/ZL3b`, `B/ZL3b`, `A/IT2a`, `B/IT2a`, `A/RF1b-e`, and `B/RF1b-e` (`how-we-built-it/calibrate_and_audit.py` and `how-we-built-it/analyze_obstructions_and_capacity.py`) isolated three structural failure mechanisms:
1. **Cardan-grille Positional-Sequential Duality Obstruction (`grille.py`)**: Under uniform random mask placement (`G0_SPLIT_RANDOM`), word draws are i.i.d. (`MI = 0.258` bits is pure finite-sample plug-in bias, excess MI $= 0.000$ bits) and prefix/suffix columns share overlapping graphemes (`E->S = 17.8% < 60%`). Under cyclic mask shifts (`G1_SPLIT_SHIFT`, `G2_SPLIT_ROTATE`, `G6_WEIGHTED_ROWS`), shifting a 3-aperture mask horizontally wraps suffix cells (`col 2, 5`) into the next word's prefix cells (`col 0, 3`), equalizing initial and final frequencies $s(g) \approx e(g)$ and collapsing `E->S` to `0.30% - 7.11%` (`0%` pass rate across all modes).
2. **Slot-based Sparse-Markov Over-Concentration (`signatures_v27.py`)**: We discovered a coding bug at line 823 of `signatures_v27.py` (`st = entry[2] if len(entry) == 3 else pair_strength`), where hardcoded 3-tuples in `DEFAULT_BOUNDARY_PAIRS` silently override the `pair_strength` sweep parameter in Experiment `S5`. Fixing line 823 and sweeping `pair_strength` from `5` to `100` revealed a deeper structural tradeoff: because `DEFAULT_BOUNDARY_PAIRS` boosts only 5 target start graphemes (`q, d, s, t, k`), any boost large enough to raise 10-chunk `MI` into the manuscript window (`pair_strength >= 25`) starves the bridge graphemes (`o, a`) and pushes `E->S` above `85.2% - 96.5%`, violating Signature 1 (`E->S <= 85%` fixed / `<= 79.5%` Currier B adaptive).
3. **Unmodified Naibbe Cipher (`naibbe.py`)**: Unmodified Naibbe has no cross-word boundary state, so its `E->S` rate sits at `52.9% - 53.2%` (below the `[60%, 85%]` fixed window and Currier A adaptive window) and its initial-grapheme rank-frequency distribution is `Intermediate` (`CV = 0.72 < 0.80`) on `40/40` streams (`0%` fixed/strict-Zipf pass rate).

## 3. What worked: `VBCLC-152` (Lens 3 `cipher-model`)
To overcome the Sparse-Markov Over-Concentration obstruction within a $\le 200$-entry hand-executable table budget, we designed **`VBCLC-152`** (`object/vbclc.py`, `object/HAND_PROCEDURE.md`, **152 table entries = 16 letter-group index entries + 20 onset entries + 96 core entries + 20 coda entries**, plus **1 per-folio state bit $\sigma_f \in \{0, 1\}$**):
- **Interleaved Zipfian Macro-Classes**: Partitioning the 20 onsets and 20 codas into 4 boundary macro-classes (`c in {0, 1, 2, 3}`) where each macro-class contains every 4th global Zipfian rank ensures that word-boundary propagation (`c_in = prev_c_out` with probability $\alpha_{\text{macro}} = 0.28$ for Currier A and $0.38$ for Currier B) generates `0.431 - 0.468` bits of 10-chunk `MI` (`0.128 - 0.232` bits of collapsed-EVA excess edge MI) **without** distorting the global initial-grapheme Zipf distribution (`Zipf R^2 = 0.921 - 0.938 > 0.85`, `CV = 1.24 - 1.25 > 0.80`).
- **Calibrated Bridge Graphemes (`o, a`)**: Placing `'o'` in Onset Class 1 (`28/169` cards) and Coda Class 3 (`15/160` cards), and `'a'` in Onset Class 3 (`6/169` cards) and Coda Class 3 (`7/160` cards), keeps both `'o'` and `'a'` strictly in the neutral `ambig` class ($\frac{1}{2} \le s(g)/e(g) \le 2$), stabilizing `E->S` at `67.3% - 68.2%` across all 6 cells while the 16 non-bridge onsets and 18 non-bridge codas maintain `100%` bilateral extremity (`Se = 13-17`, `Ee = 18-20`).
- **Non-Merging Boundary Join Rule (`safe_join_word`)**: A 4-case phonological buffer vowel check prevents onset/core/coda concatenation from accidentally creating false multi-character tokens across slot boundaries (`o+k -> ok`, `a+i -> aiin`, `d+y -> dy`, `s+h -> sh`).

## 4. Why we do not claim `p1`: The Shannon-Heaps Length-Entropy-Singleton Obstruction
In `how-we-built-it/analyze_obstructions_and_capacity.py`, we proved why a 3-slot independent-core concatenative cipher cannot simultaneously satisfy `p1` (`arXiv:2608.17096`): in collapsed EVA, Currier A has mean word length $\bar{\ell}_c = 3.80$ characters ($2.80$ character transitions per word) and conditional character entropy $h_{2,c} = 2.50$ bits/char, bounding total within-word transition entropy by $\bar{H}(W) \le 2.80 \times 2.50 = 7.00$ bits, whereas achieving a singleton-type rate $V_1/V \ge 0.692$ over $N = 10,891$ tokens (`3,604` unique word types) requires effective word-type support $V_{\text{eff}} > 5,000$ ($> 12.3$ bits) concentrated into rigid multi-character stems rather than independent 3-slot combinations (which raise $h_{2,c}$ to `3.06 - 3.12` bits and $\bar{\ell}_c$ to `4.71 - 5.12`). Achieving `p1` will require replacing the 96-entry medial slot with a multi-character rigid root-stem table whose internal character transitions are restricted to the 48 deterministic EVA bigrams of the manuscript.
