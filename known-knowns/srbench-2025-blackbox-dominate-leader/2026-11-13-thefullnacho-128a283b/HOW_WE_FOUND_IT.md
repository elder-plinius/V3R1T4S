# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

The night's target was SRBench 2025 black-box, dataset 522_pm10 — picked because the leader was low (PS-Tree, median test R^2 0.3009 at median size 4996.5) and the runner-up QLattice sat only 0.0244 behind at size 35, so a good small expression had a real chance. The same target's 1028_SWD cell had already fallen to a different procedure (median 0.3911), and two earlier nights on 522_pm10 had pushed the best our procedures reached from 0.2417 (greedy dictionary) to 0.2724 (L1-path selection) — still 0.0285 short of the bar.

The first lens tonight was a beam search over single small expressions (complexity <= 12, one expression plus an affine fit per split, selected by inner-validation R^2). It landed at median test R^2 0.1684 at median complexity 22 — below even the p2 bar. The inner-validation scores (0.21–0.39) looked promising and the test scores (0.02–0.32) did not: selecting the best of ~30,000 candidates on a 75-row inner validation overfits badly, the winner's curse as predicted in the lens notes.

The second lens imported GAMs from statistics: fit an additive model by backfitting binned-mean smoothers on each training split, compress each univariate shape into at most 3 closed-form terms (hinges at train quartiles, log(1+|.|), sqrt(|.|), squares, sin/cos for the two cyclic features), then refit all coefficients jointly by OLS. The compression is near-lossless — the shapes are simple — and the data-adaptive shapes beat the fixed dictionary: median test R^2 0.3313, median complexity 145.5.

Along the way the run caught three of its own bugs, all in reporting or plumbing, none in the scored numbers: the beam lens unpacked its best-tuple wrongly (crashed on a print after split 1; the split itself had scored correctly); the GAM lens formatted numpy float64 coefficients into expression strings, which sympy cannot parse (fixed by converting to plain floats); and the break-it script's first version read the terms count instead of the expression from the output file (its 30 "mismatches" were its own regex bug — the corrected check reproduces the claim exactly, worst |dR2| = 0.0). The panel also caught a packaging gap: the claim folder was missing read_file.py, which SRBench's symbolic_utils.py imports — fixed before the gate, and the packaged checker now runs standalone.

The claim went to a three-skeptic panel (three fresh contexts): reproduction, novelty (including a live re-fetch of the table), and criterion. All three passed. The margin is +0.0304 on the median, with 21 of 30 splits above the bar.
