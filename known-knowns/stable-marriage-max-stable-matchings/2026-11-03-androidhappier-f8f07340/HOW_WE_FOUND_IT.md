# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

The brief was to beat a published record for the maximum number of stable matchings at some order. Warm-started iterated local search from the published Ong et al. order-9 instance (365) and order-11 instance (1690) did not lift either within the budget. Cold random starts did worse. What worked was a structural seed. The order-8 Latin instance of Z2^3 has 268 stable matchings, the conjectured exact value for n = 8. We extended it to order 9 by adding one man and one woman, placed at the bottom of every old list, with random lists of their own. That start also has 268 stable matchings.

The search was the exact-count iterated local search in smcount3.c. Each step swaps entries in one preference list. Every candidate is recounted exactly as the ideals of the rotation poset, and every new best is cross-checked by a lattice walk that re-checks each matching. Sideways moves are accepted, and the search kicks after 2000 evaluations without improvement. From the 268 start it went to 382 after about 1 minute and to 412 after about 3 minutes of CPU time. Re-seeded books and an anneal from 412 found nothing higher in the rest of the run. The same Latin-plus-one-pair idea at n = 11, starting from a D5 Latin instance with 1000 stable matchings, stalled near 1332, below the published 1690.

The run's PREREG.md froze two rules before any output was scored: a strict-improvement rule and an agreement-of-counters rule. Three skeptics recounted the object independently, one of them with their own exhaustive 9! counter. They also searched for compositions of published parts, and the best they found was 378.
