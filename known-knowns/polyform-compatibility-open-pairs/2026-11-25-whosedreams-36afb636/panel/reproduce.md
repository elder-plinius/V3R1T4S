## 4. Soundness of the encoding (py/sat_compat.py, py/cnfutil.py, py/shapes.py)
- Model: one variable per board cell (f), one per M placement (p), one per X placement (q). The constraints are:
  - exactly 7k f variables are true;
  - exactly k p and exactly k q variables are true;
  - for every cell, f is true iff some p covers the cell, with at most one p covering it; the same holds for q.

  Any figure of area 7k inside the board that k M's and k X's both tile satisfies this. Connectivity and holes are not constrained, which is a relaxation, so UNSAT is stronger: it covers disconnected figures too.
- exactly_k (Sinz sequential counter for at-most-k, plus at-most-(n-k) on the negated literals): I checked it exhaustively with CaDiCaL for every n <= 7, every k and all 2^n inputs. That is 1,792 cases, and none was wrong.
- Placements: I wrote my own generator. It maps each cell to its 3 lattice vertices in axial coordinates (I checked that this mapping preserves edge adjacency on an 8x8 patch). It then applies all 12 lattice symmetries exactly with integer maps (rot60 (a,b)->(-b,a+b), mirror (a,b)->(b,a)) and every translation. For all 19 boards (k = 2..20), **the placement sets equal the encoder's P and Q exactly as sets**, not just in count. M has 6 distinct aspects (mirror-symmetric) and X has 4 (3-fold, chiral). So no placement is missing.
- The package's checker (check_mx.py with indep_compat.py) uses its own 3-coordinate system and its own symmetry code, but it compares only counts. My set comparison closes that gap.
- The object's M and X cells equal data/heptiamonds.json M and X. Letter identity is the novelty skeptic's question.
