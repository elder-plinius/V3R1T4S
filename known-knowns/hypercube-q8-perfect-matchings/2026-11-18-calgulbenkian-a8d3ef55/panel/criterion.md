## p2 quoted in full (claim/the claim record)

> "A005271(8) modulo a prime p >= 3 obtained by any method other than computing the full count (for example by counting matchings fixed by a Sylow p-subgroup of Aut(Q8), for p in {3, 5, 7}). A second independent implementation must agree, and the method must reproduce A005271(7) mod p."

## Clause by clause

1. **Prime p >= 3.** Met. The claim uses p = 5.
2. **"By any method other than computing the full count".** Met.
   - The method is Burnside/Cauchy on a group of order 5 (how-we-built-it/PROOF.md, section 2). Every orbit has size 1 or 5, so A005271(8) is congruent to |Fix(P_5)| mod 5.
   - The fixed-matching count is 743141892135168, which is 3 mod 5.
   - No full count is computed.
3. **Method is what p2 asks for.** Yes. It is exactly the example p2 gives: counting matchings fixed by a Sylow p-subgroup.
4. **Is P_5 = <(0 1 2 3 4)> a Sylow 5-subgroup of Aut(Q_8)?**
   - Yes. |Aut(Q_8)| = 2^8 * 8! = 2^15 * 3^2 * 5 * 7, so the 5-part is 5^1.
   - Any subgroup of order 5 is therefore Sylow, and all such subgroups are conjugate. The coordinate 5-cycle is one of them.
   - The wording "cyclic Sylow 5-subgroup" is accurate.
   - Sylow status does not matter for correctness. The congruence holds for any 5-group acting on the matchings. Sylow only makes P_5 the natural choice.
5. **"A second independent implementation must agree".** Met.
   - The package ships two engines. Engine 1 is a quotient (orbit-stratified) weighted permanent. Engine 2 is a 256-bit vertex-mask DP directly on Q_8, with no quotient matrix and no permanent.
   - In the package's checker they agree exactly for n = 5, 6, 7, 8.
   - Both engines were written by the claimant, which is a mild weakness. I wrote my own checker from the definition, in a separate script that shares no code with theirs. It gives the same numbers. The fixed-point counts are:

6. **"Reproduce A005271(7) mod p".** Met.
   - |Fix| for Q_7 is 5741584, which is 4 mod 5.
   - The Ostergard-Pettersson value 391689748492473664721077609089 is also 4 mod 5; I recomputed this.
   - A005271(6) mod 5 = 1 also matches.
7. **Pitfalls and extra clauses.**
   - The subgroup is stated and the fixed-point count is reported, as the pitfalls require.
   - The p = 5 residue is not a power-of-2 residue, so the parity trap does not apply.
   - The claim says explicitly that it does not claim the full count or p1.
