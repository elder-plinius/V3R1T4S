## p1: met
p1 reads: "Settle the same question for every b > 0 ...: a symbolic proof that the threshold profile from Eq. A-C is ... a Nash equilibrium."
- **Digit clause.** p1 does not inherit it. "The same question" is whether the Eq. A-C profile is a Nash equilibrium of the Section 4 game. The digit sentence is a deliverable requirement attached to "case (A) ... at b = 2". p1 states its own deliverable, a symbolic NE proof, with no value condition. Its apparent purpose, confirming that the solver has the paper's object, is met anyway: the exact cutoffs agree with the paper's printed ones to about 4e-15.
- **Game and information structure.** The game is simultaneous: each caller's payoff is computed against the other caller's fixed hand-only threshold, and nobody observes the other caller's action. Payoffs match the paper's integrals (fold -1; lone winning caller b+2; winner against two callers 2(b+1); loser -(b+1); I wins +2 if both fold).
- **Well-defined profile.** I checked the existence and uniqueness argument step by step and it is correct. L(c)=sqrt(((b+3)c²-b)/3) rises from 0 to 1 on [c0,1], with c0=sqrt(b/(b+3)). R(c)=b(1-B(c))/((2b+3)c-b) has a positive denominator there, since ((2b+3)c0)²-b² = 3b(b²+3b+3)/(b+3) > 0, and it falls from a positive value to 0. The intermediate value theorem then gives exactly one crossing. Conversely, any solution with 0<A<C<B forces C>c0, B=B(C) and A=L(C)=R(C), so B<1 and C<1 follow automatically. The ordered root is unique for every real b>0, which covers the paper's integer b.
- **Best responses.** I re-derived by hand the factorisations 3(A-x)(A+x) and b(x-B)(3(x+B)-2C) for I, and the four-piece caller difference. My independent game-tree code reproduces them. The signs show that on the open hand space (0,1), the paper's literal advice is a best response at every hand value; ties occur at A, at B, and on the caller plateau [A,C]. The callers' advice need not be symmetric for this to hold.
- **Measurable deviations.** Each player has one binary decision per information set. A measurable randomized policy earns, at each hand, a convex combination of the two action payoffs, and payoffs are bounded. Bounding pointwise by the maximum and then integrating is the standard and correct argument. It covers mixed and behavioural deviations.
- **Endpoints.** The source says "uniform(0,1) hands" and writes its advice with strict intervals. If hands are taken in (0,1), the literal advice is pointwise optimal everywhere. If hands are taken in [0,1], the literal "otherwise" actions are strictly suboptimal at three null points: check at x=0, check at x=1, call at y=0. In that reading the claim's continuity-extended representative is the one shown pointwise optimal, and the ex ante Nash comparison does not change. The claim discloses this. I count it as not obstructing p1, under the source's own uniform(0,1) hand space, and the scope below records it.
- **Symbolic and complete?** Yes. The argument is a human-readable symbolic proof for symbolic b. The polynomial identities are machine-checked twice. The analytic steps (continuity, monotonicity, IVT, integration) are short and standard, and I verified them above. Nothing is left unproved.
- **Caveat outside the criterion.** Whether the result is new is not settled: the claim notes Šimelis (1977) and Sakaguchi-Sakai (1992) as unread prior art. That is for the status and novelty review.
