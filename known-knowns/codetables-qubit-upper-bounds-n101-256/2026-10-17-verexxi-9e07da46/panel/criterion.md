## Hidden-quantifier and stretched-definition hunt

- Exactly d=53 versus all d at least 53: covered, see above.
- "Stabilizer" versus "qubit codes" in CLAIM.md: the proof is for stabilizer (additive) codes, which is what the criterion and the table (additive quantum codes) concern; my confirmed_scope is limited to stabilizer codes and says nothing about non-additive codes.
- Shadow rows: the certificate needs them (removing all of them, or any single one, makes the shipped checker reject). They are valid for every stabilizer code: on 198 random stabilizer codes with n from 3 to 8 (162 of them with odd-weight stabilizer elements, where the shadow differs from the normalizer) I enumerated S, the normalizer and the shadow by brute force and found 2^(n-k) times the normalizer weight distribution equal to the Krawtchouk sum, 2^(n-k) times the shadow distribution equal to the sign-alternating Krawtchouk sum, A_j at most B_j, and shadow counts nonnegative, with no exception. This confirms the (-1)^i sign convention.
- Null model for an over-constrained LP: using the LP as stated in the checker docstring, with scipy HiGHS, on all 435 cells with n from 2 to 30 and k from 1 to n-1, the LP was feasible at the table's lower bound (a real code exists) in 435 of 435 cells, so it never rejects an existing code. At the table's upper bound plus 1 it was infeasible in 420 cells with shadow rows and 301 without them (15 cells stay feasible, where the table uses other arguments), consistent with Rains's shadow rows being the stronger ingredient.
- Null model for the checker: I built Farkas certificates by dual LP and rationalisation for six small cells the table says have no such code ([[7,1,4]], [[8,1,4]], [[9,1,4]], [[10,2,5]], [[12,4,5]], [[14,5,5]]); the shipped checker verified all six.
