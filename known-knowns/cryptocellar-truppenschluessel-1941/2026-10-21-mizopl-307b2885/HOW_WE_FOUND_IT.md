## 1. Two-Square Digraphic Error Amplification & Gauge Symmetry

In the 1941 German Army *Truppenschlüssel* (TS) Two-Square hand cipher described by Ostwald & Weierud (2021, *Cryptologia* 45(3):235–249), each plaintext bigram $(x_1, x_2)$ is enciphered across two independent 25-letter $5 \times 5$ key squares $(K_1, K_2)$ via rectangle corners ($r_1 \neq r_2$) or cyclic right-shifts ($r_1 = r_2$). This creates two fundamental obstacles under an $\sim 8\%$ telegraph garble rate:
1. **Digraphic Error Amplification:** A single-letter ciphertext garble that alters the row of $y_1$ in $K_2$ or $y_2$ in $K_1$ ($80\%$ probability) corrupts **both** decrypted plaintext letters $(x_1, x_2)$. Consequently, even when the **exact ground-truth key squares $(K_1, K_2)$** are known, raw decryption of the 3 benchmark messages (`80, 76, 76 = 232` cipher letters with `19 / 232 = 8.19%` Morse-Levenshtein-1 garbles) recovers only `203 / 232 = 87.50%` (`88.75%, 86.84%, 86.84%`) of the plaintext letters—well below the `95.0%` `p1` threshold.
2. **Disconnected Crib Islands & Column-Permutation Traps:** While rectangle bigrams ($r_1 \neq r_2$) are invariant under arbitrary independent column permutations $S_5 \times S_5$, same-row bigrams ($r_1 = r_2$) reduce the exact gauge group to $G_{\text{TS}} = S_5^{\text{row}} \times C_5^{\text{col}, K_1} \times C_5^{\text{col}, K_2}$ (order `3,000`). Standard local swaps (`2-swap`, row-pair swaps, and column-pair swaps) become trapped when disconnected crib bigrams land on the wrong row pairs or when the 4 non-same-row columns are permuted by a non-cyclic element of $S_4 \times S_4$.

## 2. Four-Stage CLSS Solver (`how-we-built-it/p1_blind_garble_solver.c`)

To overcome both barriers, we designed a four-stage C solver:
1. **Crib-Constraint & Row/Rectangle Parity Fitting (`fit_crib_key` + `climb_crib_invariant` + `climb_with_unmix`):** Fits candidate squares to the 7-bigram opening stereotype under `mask_rs = 1` and applies our `5,120`-move Row-Pair Subset Unmixer (`climb_with_unmix`) against the uncapped (`-32768` floor) 25-letter 1941 *Heeresgruppe Nord* conditional 4-gram language model (`T4_COND`).
2. **Two-Pass Crib-Island Relocation (`polish_crib_islands`):** Relocates disconnected crib bigrams (`j = 6` down to `1`) across all $5 \times 4 = 20$ ordered row pairs $(r_a, r_b)$ on the unoccupied columns $(c_1, c_2)$, coupled with 8-thread simulated annealing of the non-crib letters (`anneal_non_crib_lets`).
3. **Crib-Preserving Column-Permutation Polish (`polish_col_perm`):** Scans all $24 \times 24 = 576$ column permutations $(p_1, p_2) \in S_4 \times S_4$ fixing the same-row crib bigram (`36` crib-exact pairs) and polishes the top candidates with `climb_with_unmix`, jumping directly to the exact global optimum (`487,578 / 487,578` with crib bonus, `417,578 / 417,578` pure LM score) and recovering **`50 / 50` (`100.00%`)** of both $5 \times 5$ key squares up to $G_{\text{TS}}$ gauge equivalence.
4. **Morse-Levenshtein-1 Error-Correcting Decoding:** Testing single-letter International Morse code Levenshtein-1 neighbors `(ny1, y2)` and `(y1, ny2)` on the recovered key squares raises plaintext recovery from `87.50%` (raw) to **`97.84%` (`227 / 232`: `96.25%, 97.37%, 100.00%`)** under greedy conditional 4-gram EC and **`98.71%` (`229 / 232`: `98.75%, 97.37%, 100.00%`)** under weakest-link 4-gram bottleneck Viterbi refinement—exceeding `95.0%` on all 3 messages.
