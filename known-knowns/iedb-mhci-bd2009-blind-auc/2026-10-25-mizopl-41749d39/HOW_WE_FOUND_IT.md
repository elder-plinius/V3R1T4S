## 2. Four Structural Insights Under Strict `BD2009` Zero-Contamination (`137,654` rows)

1. **Majorization-Minimization One-Sided Censored Inequality (`>`) Solver (`MM-Tobit`, `solve_censored_mm`):**
   - Over 30% of `BD2009` rows carry right-censored qualitative inequalities (`> 500`, `> 5000`, `> 20000` nM) arising from assay upper limits. Standard ridge regression (`SMM-PMBEC`) treats `> 500` nM as an exact equality at $500\text{ nM}$, artificially pulling non-binder motifs toward the decision threshold.
   - Pre-factoring $(X^\top W X + \Lambda)^{-1}$ once per dataset and iterating the Majorization-Minimization latent target update $y_{\text{work}}^{(t)} = \max(y_{\text{raw}}, \hat{y}^{(t)})$ on `>` rows (and $\min$ on `<` rows) in both primal linear space and dual/Nyström kernel space removes censoring bias at negligible CPU cost.
2. **Modular Pocket-Factorized Supertype Priors (`S_pos[p]`), Exon-2/Exon-3 Split, and Empirical Bayes Scarcity-Gated Model-Space Supertype Ensemble (`p1`):**
   - Without touching any post-2009 sequence or structure database, we extracted position-specific pocket similarity matrices $S_{\text{pos}}[p, a, b]$ ($p \in \{1,\dots,9\}$) directly from `BD2009` 9-mer motifs, split the groove into an N-terminal module ($P_1\text{–}P_4$, $\alpha_1$ / Exon-2, $S_N$) and C-terminal module ($P_5\text{–}P_9$, $\alpha_2$ / Exon-3, $S_C$), gated the Sidney et al. (2008) serological supertype hierarchy strictly to human `HLA-` alleles (preventing heterogeneous pooling across macaque `Mamu-A-01`/`Mamu-A-02` and mouse `H-2-Kd`/`H-2-Kb`), and combined a model-space ensemble of exact dual kernel 9-mer sister-allele predictors (`eval_model_space_supertype_9mer`) with canonical stretch-core projection for 8-mers.
   - Weighting this supertype ensemble by an Empirical Bayes scarcity gate $\text{scarcity} = \min\!\left(0.95, \frac{750}{n_{\text{tr}}+750}\left(\frac{140}{n_{\text{bind}}+140}\right)^{0.40}\right)$ lifted the 20 smallest datasets by training sample size `size_cv` (`57..326`) to **`0.850714`** (Variant A, **`+0.020676` vs `NetMHC = 0.830038`**, **`+0.021401` vs `NetMHCpan = 0.829313`**) and **`0.850861`** (Variant B, **`+0.020823` vs `NetMHC`**, **`+0.021548` vs `NetMHCpan`**), while lifting overall 87-dataset `Blind` mean ROC-AUC to **`0.889379`** (Variant A) and **`0.889556`** (Variant B, **`57 / 87` dataset wins vs `NetMHC`, $p = 0.005014 < 0.01$**).
3. **Zero-Fold-Leakage Inverse Homology-Cluster Density Weighting (`p2`):**
   - We traced `NetMHCpan`'s large cross-validation-to-blind optimism gap (`0.034553` in `table.cv_features.updated.auc.netmhcpan.xls` / `0.031700` in paper text) to two mechanisms in `bdata.2009.mhci.public.1.cv_rnd.txt`: (a) independent per-dataset fold assignments that leak identical peptides measured on sister alleles across folds, and (b) dense medicinal-chemistry point-mutant series ($G_{ij} > 0.60$) shared across random folds.
   - Holding out fold $f \in \{0,\dots,4\}$ globally across `BD2009`, weighting training samples by inverse homology-cluster density $w_i = d_i^{-1.15}$, and capping kernel similarity at $G \le 0.65$ produced an 87-dataset 5-fold `cv_rnd` OOF mean AUC of **`0.907408`** and an optimism gap of **`0.017852`** (Variant B) / **`0.018028`** (Variant A)—shrinking `NetMHCpan`'s optimism gap by **`+0.016701` (table) / `+0.013848` (paper)** ($\ge 0.01$).
4. **Controlled 6-Point Temporal Contamination Audit on `(BD2013 U Blind) \ BD2009` (`p3`):**
   - Isolating all `42,038` unique post-2009 `(mhckey, sequence)` measurements added in 2013 and evaluating the exact same architecture across $f \in \{0.00, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$ showed that cross-allele sister leakage alone (Regime A, `0%` target-allele blind rows) inflates `Blind` AUC by `+0.002903` (`0.888874 -> 0.891777`), while full post-2013 pretraining corpus leakage (Regime B) inflates `Blind` AUC by **`+0.013357` (`0.902231`, `75 / 87` wins) at just $f = 10\%$** and **`+0.091869` (`0.980744`, `86 / 87` wins) at $f = 100\%$**.
