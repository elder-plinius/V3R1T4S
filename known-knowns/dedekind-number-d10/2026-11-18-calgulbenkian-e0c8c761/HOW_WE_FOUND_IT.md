## Brief and Key Observation
Of the prior congruence work on Dedekind numbers, only `arXiv:2611.13887v1` (Nov 10, 2026) uses the `p`-group lemma `|X| == |Fix(G, X)| (mod p)`, which discards all non-singleton orbits and is limited to prime moduli; its `D(10) == 106 (mod 210)` comes from that paper together with Yamamoto's parity result. `arXiv:2302.04615` uses a different method (`S_n`-classes whose size is divisible by `m`): its Theorems 7, 10 and 14 hold modulo any `m > 1`, its Examples 12 and 15 work mod 4 and mod 12, and it states no residue of `D(10)`. However, when `P_81 = C_3 wr C_3 <= S_9` (`|P_81| = 81 = 3^4`) acts on `D_n` (`n >= 9`), every `P_81`-orbit whose stabilizer has order `1` or `3` has cardinality `81` or `27 == 0 (mod 27)`. Retaining the upper order ideal `U = {H <= P_81 : |H| >= 9}` (`27` subgroups out of `50` total) and applying Mobius inversion on `U` expresses `D(n) mod 27` as an exact integer linear combination of `|Fix_{D_n}(K)| = |D_{n-9}^{B^9 / K}|` over quotient posets of size `24 <= |B^9 / K| <= 128`.

## Why `3^3 = 27` Is the Exact Subgroup-Quotient Frontier
For `mod 81` (`3^4`), the three coordinate `3`-cycles `(0 1 2), (3 4 5), (6 7 8)` enter with sum `3 * |D_7^{P_4}|`, requiring `|D_7^{P_4}| mod 27`; since `v_3(7!) = 2`, residual coordinate symmetry on the `7` fixed variables only determines `|D_7^{P_4}| mod 9` (`3 * 9 = 27`). For `mod 25` (`5^2`), `v_5(9!) = 1` prevents `C_5 x C_5` from acting on `D_9`.
