# gamma_2 >= 833267/1048576 = 0.7946653...: a lower bound on the binary Chvatal-Sankoff constant

Changes of 2026-11-08, made after the gate in panel/gate.md: wording and credits in this README; a new object/PROOF_v2.md that restates the proof with a displayed theorem and clearer notation, with the same mathematics (object/PROOF.md is unchanged, since its sha256 names this package); the archive DOI in object/CERTIFICATE.md; stricter input checks in the three checkers, rerun on the N = 17 files; and edits to process notes in panel/ and how-we-built-it/PREREG.md, listed under Corrections to the panel text. The bound, K, m and the certificate files are unchanged.

## What

gamma_2 >= m/K with m = 13332272 and K = 2^24 = 16777216, that is gamma_2 >= 13332272/2^24 = 833267/1048576 = 0.794665336609..., a deterministic lower bound from the unrefereed proof in object/PROOF.md (restated as Theorem 1 in object/PROOF_v2.md) and an exact-integer certificate at window N = 17 with 242,084,334 states (object/CERTIFICATE.md). The three certificate files total 1.7 GB and are not shipped here; they are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24051334 (published 2026-11-08, same sha256 values as object/CERTIFICATE.md). RUN_IT_YOURSELF.md also rebuilds them in about 15 minutes, and a rebuild on 2026-11-07 matched those sha256 values byte for byte. The three shipped checkers (check_a17, check_d, check_e) accept them over all states, as did a fourth, unshipped checker. As of 2026-11-07 the best rigorous binary lower bound on the status page is 0.792665992 (Heineman et al., arXiv 2024, published at ISIT 2025), unchanged since 2026-11-01; this value is above it by 0.00199934 (records in the-record-we-beat/record.md).

The method: read both random strings one character at a time, keep a window of 17 cells of the LCS dynamic-programming table as increment bits together with the 17 characters of B in the window, and let a fixed policy decide from that state whether to read the next character of A (update the row) or of B (shift the window). The total reward is the DP value at the window's left column, which is at most the length of a longest common subsequence of the characters read. The bound follows from an integer potential V on a set of 242,084,334 states that contains every start state and is closed under the policy. object/PROOF.md has the proof as filed; object/PROOF_v2.md states it as Theorem 1 with the same steps written out.

All three checkers ran over all states and accepted, including check_a17; m + 1 is refused, and three states attain the lowest value. They were rerun on 2026-11-08 after their input checks were tightened, with the same results. verify-it-yourself/RECEIPTS.md has the output.

## Check it yourself

    sh verify-it-yourself/run_checks.sh

Expected last line: PASS. A few seconds on a laptop. This checks the N = 3, 4, 5, 8 certificates with all three checkers and exact tests of the proof steps. The check of the N = 17 certificate needs the large files and is in RUN_IT_YOURSELF.md: about 15 minutes to rebuild with about 2.54 GB of memory, under a minute per checker.

## Not claimed

No optimality and no formal verification; the proof is unrefereed. Fekete's lemma and McDiarmid's inequality are cited, not re-proved. We do not claim this is the limit of the method: window 18 did not fit the 3 GB used here, and at window 17 the one wider near-tie width tried, 0.005,0.005, stopped at a 2600 MB guard; widths under 3 GB between the two are untried. The upper bound is not touched. Lueker's journal paper was not opened; his preprint of 22 July 2005 (32 pages, Internet Archive copy of his UCI page, read 2026-11-07, sha256 eafa7611c163971f871251bcb4f09629a690b488e0e4e675a724a9764def5a4b) was read, and its Figure 1 gives 0.788071 at k = 15 as its best binary lower bound. The Tartu 2026 thesis, Bukh-Cox, Tiskin and the Archivara repository were seen only as search-result summaries. Zenodo 23735681 was not downloaded, and its metadata describe upper bounds only. The Jack repository was read on 2026-11-08: since commit bb3b815 of 2026-10-26 it also has lower bounds for alphabets of 3 to 15 letters, by Lueker's method, and it has no binary lower bound. Monte Carlo rates in verify-it-yourself/sim_rate17.c carry no weight in the bound.

Different here: window 17 in place of 16, a certificate format that goes past window 16, and a producer that fits window 17 in about 2.54 GB. Against the published work: the state, dynamic-programming frontier increments plus a window of B, is the kind of state Lueker's upper-bound automaton uses (credited above), where it decides canonicity. Published lower-bound work uses other states: Deken a pointer pair, Dancik buffers of characters read but not yet used, and Lueker and Heineman et al. pairs of length-l strings with a vector of DP values. The new part is using the increment-plus-window state on the lower side, with a policy that chooses at each step whether to read A (ROW) or B (SHIFT), and certifying it with an integer potential. The adaptive read (Dancik) and the potential certificate (Lueker's Observation 1) are earlier ideas on their own. We found no published lower bound using such a state.
