## 4. Q3: were the theorems re-derived in writing? The panel record before me did not do it fully; I did it here

DERIVATION.md states the formulas and says it "cannot replace the proofs"; the reproduce report says "re-derived by reading and proof-checking" with a one-line sketch. That is not a written re-derivation, so I wrote one. Notation: u ~ v means equal or adjacent; N(S) closed neighbourhood; the strong product has (u,v) ~ (u',v') iff u ~ u' and v ~ v'. A gadget is (I, private pairs (r_i, q_i), P^H, P^V, X) with X independent and X meet N(P^H) meet N(P^V) empty. Let R be the centres, Q the private neighbours. P^H union P^V = R union Q (each pair has one endpoint in each transversal, transversals contain only endpoints), |P^H| = |P^V| = t. Two basic facts: (F1) B = I minus R is not confusable with any endpoint (a centre differs from b inside the independent I; for q_i, N(q_i) meets I only in r_i). (F2) X0 is not confusable with any endpoint; X^H (X in N(P^H)) is not confusable with P^V; X^V is not confusable with P^H (from the empty triple intersection).

### Lemma 1 (Gao's product)
I12 = (B1 x B2) union A^H union A^V, with A^H = {(p, x): x in X2, p in L1(x)}, L1(x) = R1, P1^H, P1^V for x in X20, X2H, X2V, and A^V = {(y, q): y in X1, q in K2(y)}, K2(y) = R2, P2^V, P2^H for y in X10, X1H, X1V.
Independence. Inside each piece: fixed second coordinate and distinct first coordinates lie in one of R1, P1^H, P1^V (independent); distinct x, x' in X2 are not confusable. B1 x B2 against A^H: by F1 the first coordinates are not confusable (p lies in R1 union Q1); against A^V by F1 in the second coordinate. A^H against A^V, a point (p, x) and a point (y, q), assume p ~ y and x ~ q. If x in X20: p in R1 and q must be an endpoint-type point or in R2; x in X20 is confusable with no point of R2 union Q2 (F2), so q cannot exist unless the case is empty; and if y in X10, p (an endpoint) is not confusable with y (definition of X10). If x in X2H (p in P1^H): y in X10 gives p !~ y; y in X1H gives q in P2^V and x in X2H is not confusable with P2^V (F2); y in X1V gives y !~ P1^H (F2) so p !~ y. x in X2V is symmetric. These cases show no two points of the union, equal or distinct, are confusable, so the pieces are also disjoint and sizes add: |B1 x B2| = (a1-t1)(a2-t2), |A^H| = t1 s2, |A^V| = s1 t2: a12 as in eq. 22.
Private pairs ((r_i, x), (q_i, x)) for x in X20 and ((y, r_j), (y, q_j)) for y in X10. (r_i, x) is in I12 since L1(x) = R1. (q_i, x) is not in I12 (q_i not in I1, so not in B1 x B2 or A^H; in A^V it would force y = q_i in X1H or X1V with K2(y) a transversal of G2, but x in X20 is in no transversal). Confusable members of I12: not B1 x B2 (F1), in A^H only (r_i, x) (since x' ~ x forces x' = x by independence of X2, and q_i is confusable with only r_i in I1), none in A^V (the second coordinate would lie in R2 union Q2, F2). Endpoint-disjointness: first coordinates of the two families lie in R1 union Q1 versus X10 (X10 contains no endpoint). So t12 = t1 o2 + o1 t2.
Transversals P12^H = (P1^H x X20) union (X10 x P2^V), P12^V = (P1^V x X20) union (X10 x P2^H): one endpoint of every pair each, independent (cross terms: P1^H against X10 not confusable by definition of X10).
Decomposition of X12 = X1 x X2. A point (u, w) is in N(P12^H) iff (u in N(P1^H) and w in N(X20)) or (u in N(X10) and w in N(P2^V)); with u in X1, w in X2 and X2 independent this is (u in X1H, w in X20) or (u in X10, w in X2V). Likewise N(P12^V): (u in X1V, w in X20) or (u in X10, w in X2H). The two never hold together, so the gadget axiom holds, with neutral part (0,0) plus the four (H or V) x (H or V) combinations, H part X1H x X20 plus X10 x X2V, V part X1V x X20 plus X10 x X2H. This gives o12, h12, v12 of eq. 22. QED.

### Theorem 1 (heterogeneous auxiliary set)
Keep I12, pairs and transversals of Lemma 1 (its proof used only the gadget structure of the pairs and transversals, not the auxiliary sets, except for the last paragraph). Replace the auxiliary set by Xhet = (J0 x X20) union (JH x X2H) union (JV x X2V), J0, JH, JV independent sets of the left graph, J0 meeting N(P1^H) and N(P1^V) in no common point (33). Independence: each piece is a product of independent sets; points of different pieces have distinct points of the independent set X2 as second coordinates. Class membership, using N(P12^H) as above but with the first coordinate u now arbitrary, and the left neutral set X_L^0 = X10 of the original left auxiliary set:
- (u, w), w in X20: w is in N(X20) but in no N(P2 transversal), so (u, w) is in N(P12^H) iff u in N(P1^H), in N(P12^V) iff u in N(P1^V). By (33) never both: neutral, H, V according to the class of u in J0.
- (u, w), w in X2H: w is not in N(X20) (distinct elements of independent X2), not in N(P2^V), is in N(P2^H). So not in N(P12^H); in N(P12^V) iff u in N(X_L^0). Hence neutral if u is in JH minus N(X_L^0) (q_H points), class V otherwise (jH - q_H points).
- (u, w), w in X2V: symmetric; neutral for q_V points, class H for jV - q_V.
Never both neighbourhoods, so Xhet is a valid auxiliary set. Counting gives eqs 38/39: s = j0 oR + jH hR + jV vR, o = o0 oR + qH hR + qV vR, h = h0 oR + (jV - qV) vR, v = v0 oR + (jH - qH) hR. The proof uses about the right gadget only that X_R is independent and split into three classes, so the right gadget may itself be a Theorem 1 output. Reversing orientation (sigma) just swaps the names of P^H and P^V, so it swaps h and v. QED.

### Phi and Proposition 1
Phi: the seven families are B, X0, X^V, X^H, R, P^H, P^V (cardinalities a-t, o, v, h, t, t, t). Each is independent. The eleven symmetric separations of Definition 4 (B with O, H, V; N with A, D, O, H, V; A with D, H; D with V) follow from F1, F2, B and R disjoint in I, the three classes disjoint in X, R inside the transversals. (Table 2 of the paper; I re-checked each against the facts above.)
Proposition 1: for an admissible rule T and representations F(1..m) in graphs of dimensions d_i, define F_lambda^out as the union over tuples in T_lambda of the products F(1)_lambda1 x ... x F(m)_lambdam. A product of independent sets is independent. Two distinct tuples in T_lambda are separated in some coordinate by (i), so their products are not confusable (a pair of product points is confusable iff every coordinate is), hence the union is independent and the products are disjoint and the cardinalities add as sums of products. If lambda is separated from mu, (ii) separates every pair of tuples in some coordinate, so F_lambda^out and F_mu^out are separated. The coordinates may have different dimensions, since the argument is coordinatewise in the strong product. A terminal code is a set of tuples that are pairwise separated in some coordinate (that is (i) with one output family), which gives one independent set of the stated size. QED.
