# Research charter v1 (2026-10-14)

## Thesis
Use a builder's habit of testing definitions and data boundaries to search compact algorithms and public sky archives. Algorithms and sky archives were randomly drawn seeds, with same-object, metaphor and structured-family as randomly drawn method cards. Favor overlooked representations and observations whose value survives an independent exact check. These are proposed interests, not claims of prior algebra or astronomy expertise.

## Quarries

### fmm-additive-complexity-records
- Question: Can a small, already published matrix multiplication scheme use fewer additions under its own coefficient class and counting convention?
- Shape: Explicit straight-line program, symbolically equal to matrix multiplication, with a strictly lower addition count for one format and rank.
- Where defined: bank id `fmm-additive-complexity-records`; Perminov's FastMatrixMultiplication README and addition_reduced_ZT data, https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication. Use the bank's binding criterion and the published per-format definition.
- Status to confirm first: Latest minimum addition count, exact rank, coefficient class, and competing scheme directories. A headline rank improvement is a different question.
- Why us: A compact evaluator and careful graph rewrites let implementation discipline produce a decisive result without pretending to solve a famous rank problem.
- Weight: 5
- Wild: no

### asteroid-precovery-arc-extension
- Question: Can older public survey frames recover a known asteroid before its current recorded observation arc?
- Shape: Explicit observation bundle with two detections, astrometry, covariance propagation, static-sky exclusion and orbit-refit residuals meeting every bank condition.
- Where defined: bank id `asteroid-precovery-arc-extension`; JPL Small-Body Database, https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/sbdb.api ; Minor Planet Center observation records and public archive image identifiers named in the bank.
- Status to confirm first: Object's live first observation, full observation record, archive access and image epochs. A known or already ingested epoch has no novelty.
- Why us: Treat archive intersection, identifier reconciliation and evidence preservation as an engineering problem. Begin by reproducing recorded observations before searching missing ones.
- Weight: 2
- Wild: no

### new-periodic-variables-not-in-vsx
- Question: Can two independent public surveys confirm periodic stars absent from every catalog required by the published definition, or demonstrate existing period aliases?
- Shape: Frozen light-curve analysis and catalog-response bundle for the bank's stated batch, or its explicitly enumerated calibration or correction result.
- Where defined: bank id `new-periodic-variables-not-in-vsx`; AAVSO International Variable Star Index, https://vsx.aavso.org/ ; Gaia DR3 and the survey catalogs explicitly enumerated in the bank.
- Status to confirm first: All catalog matches, data accessibility and whether the identical objects, classifications or corrected periods have already appeared elsewhere.
- Why us: Careful cross-catalog identity and held-out validation can expose gaps hidden by a superficially convincing periodogram. Treat an error response as unknown, never absent.
- Weight: 2
- Wild: no

### fmm-ternary-integer-ring-upgrade
- Question: Can a rational small-format multiplication construction be represented at lower ternary or integer rank, or lifted to the bank's named modular milestone?
- Shape: Explicit bilinear coefficient arrays verified by exact Brent equations in the stated ring; one format and coefficient class per result.
- Where defined: bank id `fmm-ternary-integer-ring-upgrade`; Perminov's FastMatrixMultiplication coefficient-class table and scheme definitions, https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication ; Lille FMM catalogue, https://fmm.univ-lille.fr/ .
- Status to confirm first: Live ZT, Z and Q ranks, existing scheme directories, current overall rank and excluded formats.
- Why us: Explore the strange hypothesis that representation restrictions expose useful algebraic freedom. Exact coefficient checking makes the hypothesis cheaply falsifiable before larger search.
- Weight: 1
- Wild: yes

## Lenses

### evaluator-first: Make a failed candidate cheap
You derive an exact evaluator from the published definition, reproduce a named reference object, and reject corrupted examples. Profile evaluation before enlarging the search. Kill this approach if the count cannot be reconciled with the reference convention or if evaluations cost more than the intended search can sustain.
- Skeptic: no

### graph-rewrite: Compile a construction differently
You take compilation as a literal metaphor: turn a linear program into a dependency graph and search equivalent factorizations, shared subexpressions and reversible substitutions. Start with complete enumeration of tiny rewrite neighborhoods. Kill the current family when every local move is exactly tested and yields no improvement. Do not infer global optimality.
- Skeptic: no

### same-object: Reconcile different names
You ask whether the same algebraic construction, source or observation is already cataloged under another convention or identifier. Normalize dimensions, signs, transposes, coordinates and epochs. Kill a proposed novelty claim when an existing equivalent artifact covers it.
- Skeptic: yes

### structured-family: Restrict until completeness is possible
You define a small family of factorization or lifting patterns before measuring, enumerate its cheapest cases, and retain exact witnesses. For astronomy, define a bounded sample and fixed association rules before scoring. Kill the family when its whole stated finite space fails, and describe only that space.
- Skeptic: no

### boundary-audit: Look for a result manufactured by the interface
You attack coefficient counts, alias conventions, catalog errors, missing epochs and selection leakage before interpreting a promising result. Try negative controls that retain the nuisance structure. Kill the line when agreement disappears under independent data or the actual criterion excludes it.
- Skeptic: yes

## Scout voice
Read as a patient implementer. Extract definitions, units, coefficient domains, identifiers and counting conventions before adopting a headline. Distinguish a source's historical claim from its current table. Prefer a small example that can fail decisively.

## Attacker voice
Work in small experiments with captured outputs. Be curious about neglected representations and join failures. Accept a clean negative answer and retain the exact next test when a line remains live. Do not confuse familiarity with an interface with expertise in its underlying science.

## Heuristics
- Reproduce an existing object before improving it.
- Compare one format, coefficient class or celestial object at a time.
- Change representation before multiplying search volume.
- Separate novel evidence from a new label for old evidence.
- Spend effort on evaluators when they unlock many decisive experiments.
- Cross-check every apparent astronomical novelty against the catalogs named in its definition.
- Treat calibration as calibration, never as discovery.

## Kill criteria
- The published criterion is not computable from available evidence.
- An equivalent artifact is already known under another name.
- A claimed count depends on a different convention or coefficient class.
- Independent survey evidence or orbit consistency contradicts the signal.
- A proposed finite family cannot fit the available computation or storage.
- A result survives only after selecting thresholds from its own measured outcome.

## Self-critique
This charter may overestimate how far software evaluation habits transfer into algebra or astronomy. Archive access and mature competitors may consume whole runs without leaving a new object. The random seeds prevent a conventional choice; they do not establish a competitive advantage. Keep the initial problem small enough to expose whether the evaluator and comparison convention are sound.

## Method cards used
- same-object: algebraic equivalence and cross-catalog identity.
- metaphor: compile an existing construction as a dependency graph.
- structured-family: a finite, explicit family with exact witnesses and limited conclusions.
