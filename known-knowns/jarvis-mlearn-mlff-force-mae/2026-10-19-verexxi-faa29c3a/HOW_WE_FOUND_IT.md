Brief: beat the best listed Mo force MAE (snap_mlearn, 0.18434 eV/A) on the 23 official test structures by at least 5%, using only the 194 train structures, no pretraining. The Mo set is 54-atom bcc cells: roughly the first 130 structures are ab initio MD snapshots with large forces (typically 0.5 to 2 eV/A per component, some above 4), the rest are strained, defected or small cells with forces near zero. The official test ids are a stratified draw from the same groups, so the zero-force MAE is high (0.95 eV/A). First step: recompute the leaderboard metric from the published predictions of two listed entries and match their listed values to every digit. Then a validation split was carved out of train (every tenth id) because the benchmark json has only train and test. What did not work: a first MACE config (16 channels, batch 10, learning rate 0.01, 120 epochs) reached a val MAE of 0.1089 and was clearly underfitted (train and val error equal after only about 2000 optimizer steps); a 32-channel run at the same settings was stopped early for the same reason. What worked: batch size 3 with learning rate 0.005 for 150 epochs; the 16-channel version scored 0.1054 and the 32-channel version 0.1048 on val, so the 32-channel one won under the frozen rule. Three seeds were then trained on all 194 train ids and the test set was scored once: seeds 0.1063, 0.1041, 0.1066, ensemble 0.0974. The listed SNAP entry is a linear model; a small equivariant network on 30,000 force components simply fits the MD snapshots much better. The models are poor on energies (energy RMSE tens of meV/atom), so energies are not claimed. A later paper (ALIGNN 2.0) lists 0.1121 for this task; it was found after the models were trained and does not change the result.
