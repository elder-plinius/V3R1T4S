# How we found it (contributor text, unverified)

**Brief.** Charter quarry 5 asks to extend the exact census of anomaly-free U(1) charge assignments of the SM
fermions, with and without three right-handed neutrinos, beyond the largest Q_max tabulated by Allanach, Davighi
and Melville, with the full solution list as the certificate. The run (2026-10-18) could not
reach arXiv, Springer or Zenodo (proxy 403), so the published values it had were search-tool text (SM Q_max 1, 3, 5,
7, 10; SMnuR 6..10) and the paper's word "inequivalent" had to be pinned by calibration. The pre-registration
(`how-we-built-it/PREREG.md`, frozen 05:56:28 UTC before any attack output) required: one convention reproducing
every published value; two enumerators with different algorithms, one of them listing; the checker passing on the
full list; nothing claimed for an unfinished bound.

**What worked.** Four lenses ran in parallel.
- *solver*: two raw counters (`count.c`, sort-merge over the halves (Q,u,d) | (L,e,nu); `count2.c`, a hash table over
  (Q,L,e) | (u,d,nu)) count every tuple of sorted species triples that satisfies the six conditions; `fix.py` and
  `conventions.py` turn that into candidate conventions (Burnside for the overall sign, Moebius for common factors).
  One convention, 1 + primitive nonzero classes modulo independent family permutations per species and the overall
  sign, reproduced every published value. SMnuR Q_max 10 took 13.4 s (the paper reports 24748 s for its program).
- *falsify-first*: `acclist.c` lists the canonical solutions directly (meet in the middle, output already sorted),
  and the checker `check_list.py` verifies every line against the definition. Published count = list lines + 1 at
  all ten published points. Lists for SM Q_max 11 (37005 lines) and SMnuR Q_max 11 (45378377 lines) passed the
  checker; the line counts + 1 equal the counters' 37006 and 45378378. A Python brute force agreed line for line at
  Q_max 1..3.
- *already-settled*: 25+ searches found no count at Q_max >= 11; the convention also reproduced the paper's
  "Symmetry" (charge-inversion-symmetric) column at the three points quoted by the search tool.
- *same-object*: a sorry-free Lean proof that the six equations used here have exactly the solution set of
  Physlib's formal `SMRHN.PlusU1 3` (and the SM system is its nu = 0 restriction); Physlib's permutation group is
  the same independent S3 per species.

**What failed.** Every other convention missed the published values: counting F and -F separately (SMnuR Q_max 10:
43086299 against 21546920), keeping solutions with a common factor (21663084), excluding the zero assignment (every
value off by one), one family permutation acting on all species at once (SM Q_max 1: 728 classes against 8). A C
run of SM Q_max 10 in the same-object lens was stopped because its table would have needed about 13 GB, and a Lean
brute force at SM Q_max 3 hit its 30-minute limit before a smaller rerun finished.

**Panel.** REPRODUCE (own counter, own checker, both lists regenerated byte-identically) and CRITERION passed on
2026-10-18; NOVELTY could not read the table and returned PENDING STATUS CHECK (`panel/novelty_round1_pending.md`),
so the gate wrote PROMISING. On 2026-10-23 the workspace owner supplied the paper (v5) and the erratum as PDFs; a
fresh NOVELTY skeptic read them (largest published Q_max is 10 for both contents) and passed, all 40 published numbers
were matched by the counters, and the gate's second round wrote CONFIRMED_SOLUTION (`panel/gate.md`).
