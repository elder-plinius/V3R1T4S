## 1. Why the Baseline Stopped at $n = 129$ (`KNOWN`)

In Appendix B of *"The Reversed Zeckendorf Game"* (`arXiv:2309.12748v2`), Zoë X. Batterman, Aditya Jambhale, Steven J. Miller, Akash L. Narayanan, Kishan Sharma, Andrew K. Yang, and Chris Yao represent game states as variable-length objects storing `(piece_count, values_tuple, move_index)`, test membership via linear list scans (`if current_state in calculated_states`), and propagate win/loss labels via repeated full-graph scans (`while not_done`) for each starting integer $n$ separately in Python. They report that $n = 129$ required roughly 2 hours and $n = 144$ required roughly 24 hours, stopping their published Table A1 at $n = 129$ ($384,715$ vertices, $2,945,040$ edges).

## 2. Two Structural Observations (`PROVED-HERE`)

1. **Universal $h_1$-Eliminated Embedding & Deficit Stratification (Lens 1, `PROVED-HERE`)**:
   Every game state for a starting integer $n \le 250$ is a tuple $(h_1, h_2, \dots, h_{12}) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{12}$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{12} h_i F_i = n$ ($F_1=1, F_2=2, F_3=3, \dots, F_{12}=233$). Eliminating $h_1 = n - \sum_{i=2}^{12} h_i F_i$ embeds every game DAG $G_n$ ($2 \le n \le 250$) as the induced subgraph $V_n = \{h : m(h) \le n\}$ (where $m(h) = \sum_{i=2}^{12} h_i F_i$) of a single universal state space of $A003107(250) = 21,890,964$ states.
   Furthermore, the deficit $\Delta(h) = \sum_{i=2}^{12}(F_i - 1)h_i = n - \sum_{i=1}^{12} h_i$ is strictly invariant under all Combine moves and decreases by 1 under all Split moves, while within each constant-$\Delta$ stratum every Combine move strictly increases $(m(h), \sum_{i=2}^{12} i \cdot h_i)$. This permits a single bottom-up pass over $\Delta = 0 \dots 250$ that evaluates all $n \in [2, 250]$ simultaneously in 256-bit word-parallel bitvectors (`how-we-built-it/universal_delta_solver.cpp`; `NUMERICAL`: `9.59 s` wall time and `64,468 kbytes` peak RSS with portable `-O3 -std=c++17`, or `5.97 s`–`6.54 s` with `-march=native`).

2. **Colexicographic Topological Order & Closed-Form Edge Identity (Lens 2, `PROVED-HERE`)**:
   For any fixed $n$, every legal Split move ($F_{i+1} \mapsto F_i + F_{i-1}$ or $F_2 \mapsto 2F_1$) and every legal Combine move ($F_{i-2} + F_{i+1} \mapsto 2F_i$ or $F_1 + F_3 \mapsto 2F_2$) strictly decreases the highest modified coordinate $h_{i+1}$ by 1 while leaving all higher coordinates unchanged. Consequently, the colexicographic rank $r \in \{0, \dots, A003107(n)-1\}$ of $(h_1, \dots, h_{12})$ is a strict reverse-topological ordering of $G_n$, with $r = 0$ as the unique terminal state $(n, 0, \dots, 0)$ and $r = A003107(n)-1$ as the initial Zeckendorf decomposition of $n$. Using a partition DP offset table, `how-we-built-it/colex_rank_solver.cpp` evaluates each $n \in [2, 250]$ in two 1-bit-per-rank bitsets (`win_bits` and `reach_bits`, `2,736,376 bytes` each at $n = 250$) without any hash table or graph storage, and runs a top-down reachability pass from $r = A003107(n)-1$ confirming that all $A003107(n)$ partitions and all $\sum_{i=2}^{12}(A003107(n - F_i) + A003107(n - 2F_i))$ directed transitions are reachable (`NUMERICAL`: `23.94 s` wall time and `75,916 kbytes` peak RSS across OpenMP threads with portable `-O3 -fopenmp -std=c++17`, or `8.92 s`–`9.53 s` with `-march=native`).

## 3. Calibration Catch During Development

During initial calibration of Solver 1 against small $n$, the loop for $i \ge 3$ Combine moves ($F_{i-2} + F_{i+1} \mapsto 2F_i$) initially tested `h[i - 2] >= 1` for $i = 3..11$, which at $i = 3$ read `h[1]` (which was not populated in the $h_1$-eliminated array `h[2..12]`) instead of checking $h_1 = n - m(h) \ge 1$. Calibration at $n = 6$ immediately flagged 10 edges instead of 11. Separating the $i = 3$ Combine move ($F_1 + F_4 \mapsto 2F_3$, legal iff $h_4 \ge 1$ and $m + 1 \le n$) resolved all 128 calibration rows of Table A1 ($2 \le n \le 129$) and produced byte-identical agreement between both independent solvers across the entire range $2 \le n \le 250$.
